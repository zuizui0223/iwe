# Screening batch 005 — frozen antagonist query

Date: 2026-09-17
Scope: IWE049–IWE053

## Purpose

This batch used the frozen antagonist search logic to look specifically for studies that could falsify the emerging measurement-asymmetry diagnosis by providing all of:

```text
animal activity/emergence timing
+
plant temporal position
+
final plant reproductive fitness
+
recoverable uncertainty
```

No record in this batch closes that full chain.

## IWE049 — Hybanthus prunifolius

Augspurger (1981) is strong prior art on reproductive synchrony. It experimentally compares plant reproductive success for individuals flowering in versus out of synchrony with the plant population and tests pollinator and seed-predator effects.

The timing variable is **conspecific plant synchrony**, however, not temporal matching to an independently measured animal activity curve. It therefore remains outside strict H1.

## IWE050 — Erythrina falcata

Trees were classified as early, peak or late flowering. Mean seed-predation rates were:

- early: 65%;
- peak: 80%;
- late: 50%.

This is direct evidence of temporal escape from a seed-predator guild. The source endpoint is seed-predation rate rather than final seed/offspring fitness, and the predator guild activity curve is not an IWE overlap exposure. The study is therefore context/timing evidence, not strict H1.

## IWE051 — Ulex europaeus

A common-garden experiment demonstrates genetic variation in flowering strategies associated with reduced seed predation via temporal escape or predator satiation. This is strong evolutionary evidence that flowering phenology can reduce enemy cost, but it does not identify a partner-timing-to-final-fitness effect on the strict IWE scale.

The biological cluster hash would place this record in the reserved shape-holdout stratum, but the temporal-escape result was already visible during discovery. It is explicitly ineligible as blind confirmation.

## IWE052 / IWE053 — Lathyrus vernus long-term programme

The Lathyrus programme is unusually strong demographically:

- flowering time;
- seed set;
- predispersal seed predation;
- vertebrate grazing;
- lifetime/demographic fitness or long-term selection;
- climatic variation.

The 2021 analysis spans 21 years and directly tests whether plant–animal interactions mediate climate effects on selection on flowering time.

Nevertheless, the interaction variables are damage/intensity/covariance rather than an independently measured partner activity distribution from which plant–partner temporal overlap can be constructed. The studies therefore remain multi-agent timing/selection context rather than strict H1.

## Batch conclusion

The absence of a strict antagonist row after a search deliberately enriched for phenology + seed predation + plant fitness is informative about measurement architecture, but not yet a biological result.

The recovered studies strongly establish:

```text
seasonal timing matters for antagonist exposure and selection
```

while rarely identifying:

```text
plant-antagonist temporal matching -> final plant fitness
```

with the same exposure definition used in direct mutualist mismatch studies.
