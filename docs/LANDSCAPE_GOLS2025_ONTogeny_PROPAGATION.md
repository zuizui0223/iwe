# Gols 2025 — prospective herbivory timing can be preserved or buffered within one experiment

Date: 2026-10-06  
Study: Gols 2025, *Pieris brassicae* herbivory across host ontogeny  
DOI: `10.1111/plb.13757`  
Landscape role: direct timing-manipulation boundary showing species-specific propagation to final reproductive potential.

## Design

Caterpillar herbivory was imposed directly at source-defined host developmental stages.

For the two species with the same integrated final endpoint considered here:

- *Sinapis arvensis*: budding, flowering, and late-flowering attack;
- *Brassica nigra*: vegetative, budding, and flowering attack.

Herbivory intensity was also experimentally varied.

The final integrated endpoint, **reproductive potential**, is calculated as:

`number of seeds per plant × seed germination probability`.

This is downstream of seed production and seed quality and is therefore a stronger final reproductive endpoint than either component alone.

## Sinapis arvensis — timing signal buffered

Herbivory timing strongly changes intermediate reproductive components:

- total seed mass: ontogeny without controls, **F = 10.6, p < 0.001**;
- individual seed mass: **F = 38.0, p < 0.001**;
- germination: **F = 8.23, p = 0.016**.

But the integrated reproductive potential does **not** vary with herbivory timing:

- ontogeny without controls: statistic = **0.21, p = 0.81**.

The source shows compensatory changes in seed quantity, seed mass, and germination. The timing signal is therefore classified as **buffered**, not erased: downstream compensation is biologically identified.

## Brassica nigra — timing signal preserved

The same experimental architecture gives the opposite propagation outcome in *B. nigra*.

Final reproductive potential differs strongly among herbivory stages:

- ontogeny without controls: statistic = **41.2, p < 0.001**.

The source reports that vegetative- and budding-stage herbivory reduces reproductive potential, whereas flowering-stage herbivory produces reproductive potential similar to undamaged controls.

Here the direct timing manipulation is preserved to the final integrated endpoint.

## Why the pair matters

The two species are studied in one experiment with the same herbivore and closely comparable timing manipulations, yet the final propagation state differs.

This is a strong guardrail for the temporal-information hypothesis:

> prospective timing measurement can increase causal clarity without guaranteeing that the temporal signal survives to final fitness.

Whether the signal survives depends on downstream compensation and host biology.

## Registry treatment
