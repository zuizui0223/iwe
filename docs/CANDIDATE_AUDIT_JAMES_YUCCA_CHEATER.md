# Candidate audit — Yucca kanabensis × non-pollinating yucca moth

Date: 2026-09-25  
Source: James (1998), *Limits on the Exploitation of the Yucca–Yucca Moth Mutualism*, MSc thesis, University of Alberta  
Archive: Library and Archives Canada, MQ28950  
Candidate: `ANT002_JAMES_1998_YUCCA`  
Decision: **retain as P1 `blocked_summary_stats`**

## Why this source matters

This thesis is a rare antagonist study in which the focal seed predator's timing is measured directly rather than reconstructed from attack damage.

The source predates Pellmyr's 1999 taxonomic revision. James calls the focal antagonist an unnamed non-pollinating member of the `Tegeticula yuccasella` complex associated with *Yucca kanabensis*. Later Canadian syntheses discuss James (1998) when describing non-pollinating cheater yucca moths, including *T. corruptrix*. IWE therefore retains conservative source-level taxonomic wording rather than retroactively forcing one species label.

## Same-season timing gate

Chapter 4 is explicitly titled **Life History Synchronization and Yucca Population Structure**.

The 1995 and 1996 field programmes directly record:

- non-pollinator emergence/activity during the focal season;
- the date each study plant began flowering;
- the age/stage of fruit available when non-pollinators emerged;
- repeated oviposition activity through the antagonist flight season.

The source shows that antagonist emergence was synchronized with the availability of suitable yucca fruit in both focal years. Females emerged when fruit roughly a week old or older were available.

The timing design also generates a biologically interpretable seasonal contrast:

- earlier-flowering/non-isolated plants have older suitable fruit available when the antagonist flight period begins and receive more ovipositions;
- later-flowering plants have younger fruit and lower use at the relevant time.

This ordering is determined from observed antagonist emergence plus host phenology. It is not inferred from final seed damage.

## Post-predation reproductive endpoint

The same programme contains a direct plant reproductive-cost surface.

At the end of the focal observation period, James collected one to two fruits per plant after non-pollinator development had progressed and counted:

- intact seeds;
- damaged/destroyed seeds;
- non-pollinator ovipositions and larvae.

Chapter 2 independently shows that non-pollinating larvae consumed about 30% of seeds on average, with individual fruit ranging from no loss to very severe loss.

Thus the biological chain exists within one independent programme:

`measured antagonist emergence -> host reproductive timing -> realized seed destruction`.

## Why no SMD is extracted

The blocker is publication form.

Chapter 4 methods explicitly state that intact versus damaged seeds were counted per plant to test whether flowering day, isolation and fruit number affected seed destruction.

But the published Chapter 4 results report the timing analyses for:

- presence/probability of antagonist oviposition;
- number of ovipositions per fruit;
- presence/number of antagonist larvae.

They do **not** report an early-versus-late flowering group mean ± SD/SE for intact seed production or seed destruction, nor a timing-group test statistic from which an SMD can be recovered.

The thesis therefore cannot currently satisfy the frozen SMD family.

IWE will not:

- substitute oviposition count for final plant reproduction;
- combine Chapter 2's overall 30% seed loss with Chapter 4's flowering-date pattern;
- use fruit or locules as independent timing replicates when exposure is plant-level;
- convert a continuous flowering-date slope on an intermediate outcome into an SMD;
- assume that early versus late oviposition differences imply an equal contrast in final seed destruction.

## Full-thesis retrieval audit

The complete Library and Archives Canada PDF (MQ28950; 151 PDF pages) was recovered and text-audited on 2026-09-25. This resolves the previous archival uncertainty.

Chapter 4 methods explicitly tie the required variables to the same 1996 field programme:

- each plant's flowering start/end was recorded;
- non-pollinator oviposition activity was checked repeatedly through the flight period;
- one to two fruits per plant were collected after larval development;
- oviposition scars, larvae, intact seeds and damaged seeds were counted;
- the stated analysis plan included testing effects of flowering day, isolation and fruit number on seed destruction.

The published Chapter 4 results then report flowering-time relationships for oviposition probability, oviposition count and larval occurrence, but they do **not** report the corresponding flowering-time result for intact/damaged seeds. Searches of the thesis text and tables do not reveal an appendix containing the plant-level seed-by-date records.

Therefore the remaining blocker is no longer “retrieve the thesis.” It is specifically **recover unpublished plant-level final-seed summaries/raw data**.

## Exact unlock condition

This candidate can close antagonist #2 if the original 1995/1996 plant-level data or a companion source provides, for the predeclared more-versus-less synchronized flowering groups:

- mean intact-seed count, viable-seed count, or proportion surviving;
- SD/SE and independent plant n;

or an equivalent source-reported group test that allows the same final-seed SMD and sampling variance to be reconstructed without changing the estimand.

## Relationship to Tomares

The antagonist search now has two complementary P1 routes:

1. *Astragalus–Tomares*: final reproductive group summaries are published, but at the wrong nested sampling grain for the synchrony contrast.
2. *Yucca–non-pollinating Tegeticula*: plant-level timing and final seed damage were both measured, but the source does not publish final seed summaries stratified by timing group.

Neither should be forced across its blocker. Either original dataset would be sufficient to advance antagonist replication if the missing correct-unit summary is recovered.
