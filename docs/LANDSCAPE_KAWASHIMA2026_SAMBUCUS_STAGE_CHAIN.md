# Kawashima 2026 — larval-triggered abortion closes a mixed stage chain to viable seed

Date: 2026-10-08  
Study: Kawashima et al. 2026, *Sambucus sieboldiana × Heterhelus* spp.  
DOI: `10.1002/ppp3.70175`  
Landscape role: independent mixed-system stage-structure evidence linking pollination, larval infestation, delayed fruit abortion and final viable-seed fate.

## Why this system matters

The strict IWE partner-window audit correctly rejects this programme as a synchrony effect because adult *Heterhelus* activity is described qualitatively through anthesis rather than as a quantitative high-versus-low availability series.

The stage-specific pivot asks a different question:

> does the programme preserve the causal sequence from adult service through delayed offspring cost and host filtering to final reproduction?

For *Sambucus–Heterhelus*, the answer is yes.

## Stage chain

The source shows:

1. adult *Heterhelus* are the dominant effective pollinators;
2. larvae preferentially enter ovaries with fertilized ovules;
3. larval feeding, rather than oviposition itself, triggers fruit abortion;
4. infested fruits usually abort about one month after flowering;
5. larvae nevertheless complete development in the fallen fruits;
6. infested/aborted fruits contain few viable seeds and have low seed germination;
7. final fruit and viable-seed outcomes are measured after the abortion period.

This is a full mixed-interaction stage chain even though it is not a partner-relative timing contrast.

## Final seed consequences

The source reports a sharp endpoint difference between infested-aborted and mature fruits:

- viable seeds: **20.1%** in infested fruits versus **77.2%** in mature fruits;
- germination: **2.7%** versus **38.7%**.

Thus stopping at fertilization or fruit initiation would badly misrepresent final plant benefit.

## Context changes the final benefit-cost balance

The 2024 cost-benefit assessment gives:

- Shiga viable-seed benefit: **62.85%**;
- Awaji viable-seed benefit: **36.83%**.

Minimum-to-maximum larval seed costs are:

- Shiga: **4.97–10.64%**;
- Awaji: **8.95–26.72%**.

The resulting benefit:cost ratios are:

- Shiga: **5.91–12.65**;
- Awaji: **1.38–4.12**.

The stage architecture is similar at both sites, but resource limitation and seed viability differ enough to change the final balance strongly.

## Host filtering is unusual here

In many nursery-pollination systems, abortion kills the pollinator offspring and can act as a sanction.

Here, abortion does **not** kill the larva. The larva completes development inside the fallen fruit.

The host filter therefore mainly reduces maternal resource allocation to an already compromised reproductive unit rather than eliminating the consumer.

This is a fourth host-filter architecture in the pivot:

- host development removes future damaging exposures (Cardamine);
- host allocation deletes exposed units and kills offspring (Yucca);
- consumers predict or manipulate retention (Lathyrus, Rheum);
- larval damage triggers abortion that reduces plant investment while still preserving consumer development (Sambucus).

## Endpoint lesson

The study also strengthens the endpoint rule.

Fruit set, viable seed set and seed germination are not interchangeable. A stage-specific model should only claim plant fitness after propagating the interaction through the latest source-supported reproductive endpoint.

This parallels the Trollius 2015 boundary, where Chiastocheta presence raises net seed set but the advantage disappears when germination is included in relative female fitness.
