# IWE032 extraction receipt — Sercu et al. 2020

Source: Sercu BK, Moeneclaey I, Bonte D, Baeten L. 2020. *Induced phenological avoidance: A neglected defense mechanism against seed predation in plants*. Journal of Ecology 108:1115–1124. DOI `10.1111/1365-2745.13325`.

Data archive: Dryad `10.5061/dryad.x3ffbg7dz`.

Status: **antagonist shape evidence; not a single linear H1 effect**.

## Why this system is unusually useful

The host `Geum urbanum` is mainly self-pollinating, which strongly reduces the usual problem that early/late flowering changes both pollination benefit and enemy exposure. The specialist predispersal seed predator `Byturus ochraceus` has a restricted seasonal window, while the plant flowers from early summer into autumn.

The source explicitly defines off-peak flowering relative to predator activity as the proportion of a plant's flowers produced in the second flowering peak.

Thus the biological timing axis is directly tied to antagonist exposure rather than inferred from calendar date alone.

## Matched timing and fitness observations

The source reports:

- two flowering peaks, around late June and mid-August;
- seed predation almost entirely in the first peak / June–early July;
- >50% of monitored plants experiencing predation in each focal year;
- total seed mass per flower and total seed mass per plant as reproductive-output measures.

At flower level in 2015, seed mass declined over date in unpredated plants (`-0.0027 g/day`, SE `0.0002`), predation reduced early-flower seed mass (`-0.11 g`, SE `0.025`), and the predation × flowering-date interaction was positive (`+0.0013`, SE `0.00027`).

## Plant-level fitness shape

For unpredated plants, no significant relationship between off-peak flowering and total seed mass was supported.

For predated plants, a quadratic model was favored over a linear model and all fitted terms were reported as significant. Predicted total seed mass was highest when approximately `36%` of flowers occurred in the second/off-peak flowering period.

This is not a monotone `more synchrony -> fitness` relationship. It is direct evidence that the timing-fitness surface under an antagonist can have an interior optimum because complete escape into the late period carries its own seasonal cost.

## IWE classification

IWE032 is therefore assigned:

- interaction class: `antagonist`;
- evidence role: `include_shape`;
- phenology source: direct seasonal enemy window;
- fitness outcome: seed mass / total seed mass;
- strict H1: **not admitted**;
- screening-informed shape lane: **admitted**.

No linear coefficient is created by collapsing the first and second flowering peaks.

## Claim boundary

IWE032 supports the descriptive statement that antagonist exposure can generate a nonlinear timing-fitness surface when phenological escape itself has a reproductive cost.

It does not establish that antagonist systems generally have an intermediate optimum. That generalization was recognized during screening and requires either a descriptive discovery synthesis or prospective confirmation in a held-out system set.
