# IWE hypotheses

## H1 — interaction-type moderation (primary)

The study-level reproductive effect of increased phenological synchrony differs among three preregistered interaction classes:

- `mutualist`
- `antagonist`
- `mixed_pollinating_seed_predator`

All effects are oriented so positive means greater synchrony is associated with higher plant reproductive performance. The primary inferential target is heterogeneity among classes, not a universal pooled synchrony effect.

## H2 — non-monotonic synchrony in mixed interactions (primary extension)

Within `mixed_pollinating_seed_predator`, net reproductive performance may peak at intermediate synchrony because synchrony can increase both pollination benefit and offspring-mediated reproductive cost.

Curvature is evaluated only when independent mixed-system evidence spans enough of the synchrony axis. Failure to estimate or support curvature is a valid result.

## H3 — redundancy buffering (secondary)

Where measured independently of the focal reproductive outcome, greater effective-partner redundancy may reduce reproductive loss associated with mismatch.

## Secondary moderators

Predeclared secondary moderators are partner specialization/obligacy, effective partner redundancy, latitude, elevation, climatic seasonality, island/mainland context, plant life history, observational versus experimental timing, and direct versus proxy phenology source.

Secondary moderators do not redefine H1 or rescue an unsupported primary result.
