# Eureka 2001 squarrose-knapweed public-route audit

Date: 2026-09-28  
Candidate: `ANT002_KNAPWEED_LARINUS_2001`  
System: *Centaurea virgata* ssp. *squarrosa* × *Larinus minutus* / *Urophora quadrifasciata*  
Decision: **public search exhausted; retain P2 `blocked_final_surface`, move to archive retrieval**

## Why this programme was attractive

The Eureka, Utah programme satisfies the timing side unusually well for an antagonist candidate.

Evans (2016), *Biodiversity, ecosystem functioning, and classical biological control* (Applied Entomology and Zoology 51:173–184; DOI `10.1007/s13355-016-0401-z`) reports that in 2001:

- adult *U. quadrifasciata* and *L. minutus* abundance was measured with 100 sweep-net samples once per week from late May through early September;
- after flowering began in early July, up to 200 open knapweed flowers were marked at each weekly visit using a cohort-specific string colour;
- marked flowers were collected as mature seed heads 4–6 weeks later and dissected;
- the timing of seed-head infestation mirrored the independently observed adult phenology.

Thus the partner-activity window is not inferred from damage alone, and each marked flower carries a prospective flowering cohort into a mature reproductive structure.

## Publicly recoverable outcome surface

The 2016 synthesis publishes Figure 2 as:

- weekly adult fly/weevil abundance; and
- percentage of marked flowers from each date that later became seed heads infested by flies or weevils.

It does **not** publish, by weekly marked-flower cohort:

- mature viable seed number;
- intact seed number;
- mean seed production;
- SD/SE/variance of seed production; or
- a raw marked-head table from which those values can be calculated.

The text states only that the mature seed heads were dissected to determine their contents. Dissection itself is not evidence that final viable-seed counts were retained in an accessible quantitative table.

## 2006 programme abstract

The Western Society of Weed Science 2006 proceedings contain the abstract:

> Community Impact of Seed Head Insects on Reproductive Success of Squarrose Knapweed in Utah

by Edward W. Evans, Trent R. Toler, Julie P. Rieder, and T. A. Scott Newbold.

This source independently confirms that:

- sweep samples of adults and fates of individually marked flowerheads were used;
- flies exerted early/late-season pressure while *L. minutus* attacked mainly mid-season;
- programme-level impact on weed reproductive success and seed production was assessed during 2000–2005.

But the abstract reports only broad site/year impact percentages and narrative reproductive consequences. It does not provide a weekly Eureka 2001 cohort table with final seed means and variance.

## 2001 Rieder et al. paper

Rieder, Evans & Durham (2001), *Distribution of Insect Attacks in Biological Control of Weeds: Infestation of Centaurea virgata Flowerheads by a Gall Fly* (Biological Control 20:254–260; DOI `10.1006/bcon.2000.0901`) is useful programme context.

It reports earlier west-central Utah site/year dissections and relates gall aggregation to mean flowerhead quality (mean seeds per flowerhead). However, those data precede the focal 2001 Eureka weekly phenology experiment and do not supply the required flowering-cohort × adult-activity × final-seed surface.

They therefore cannot be spliced into the Eureka timing series.

## Other public searches

The public search also checked:

- Utah State University indexed graduate/repository records;
- indexed publications and abstracts associated with Edward W. Evans, Julie P. Rieder, Trent R. Toler, and T. A. Scott Newbold;
- later Utah/USDA biocontrol reports;
- California squarrose-knapweed annual reports.

Some non-Eureka sources report seed destruction or seeds per uninfested head, but those are different sites, years, and sampling programmes. They are not admissible substitutes for the marked 2001 Eureka cohorts.

## Exact remaining unlock

The programme becomes SMD-ready only if one of the following is recovered:

1. the original 2001 Eureka marked-flower field/laboratory table;
2. a source-backed weekly cohort summary derived from that table; or
3. another programme archive object that preserves the same marked-head unit.

The minimum required fields are:

- flowering cohort/date;
- marked-head identity or a valid cohort summary;
- final mature viable-seed / intact-seed production;
- sample size and variance-bearing information.

The independently measured adult phenology can then prospectively order the flowering cohorts before reproductive values are inspected.

## Stop rule

Do not:

- repeat generic public-web searching of the same programme;
- use weekly infestation percentage as plant final reproduction;
- infer final seeds from the statement that heads were dissected;
- splice California seed-destruction values into the Eureka experiment;
- splice other Utah sites/years into the focal 2001 timing contrast;
- define high/low timing groups using seed outcomes.
