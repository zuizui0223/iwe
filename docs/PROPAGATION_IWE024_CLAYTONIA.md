# IWE024 Gezon et al. 2016 — one flowering-timing experiment preserves and reverses the pollination signal

Date: 2026-10-07  
Study: Gezon, Inouye & Irwin 2016, *Claytonia lanceolata*  
DOI: `10.1111/gcb.13209`  
Propagation role: direct flowering-time manipulation with two source-defined downstream fates.

## Design

The programme manipulates flowering phenology in two ways:

1. snow removal versus natural snowmelt in 2011–2013;
2. a 2013 outdoor-array experiment with greenhouse-induced **early, control and late** flowering treatments.

The array experiment is the cleanest propagation component because flowering timing is directly imposed without changing snow/soil conditions.

The source measures:

- pollinator visitation;
- pollen limitation;
- fruit set;
- seed set;
- frost damage.

The response is therefore downstream plant reproduction, not only visitation.

## Early versus control — biotic signal reversed by abiotic damage

Early-array plants flower roughly 10 d ahead of controls.

They receive high pollinator visitation and are not pollen limited, so the pollination-service pathway by itself predicts good reproduction.

However, early plants experience severe frost damage and **almost completely fail to reproduce**.

Thus the upstream biotic timing signal does not survive to the final endpoint:

`early flowering -> high pollinator visitation / adequate pollen -> severe frost -> very low reproduction`.

This link is classified as **sign_reversed** relative to the pollination-service signal.

The mechanism is source identified rather than inferred from a non-significant outcome: frost damage directly overwhelms the pollination benefit.

## Late versus control — pollination signal preserved

Late-array plants flower after the control window.

They receive low pollinator visitation, are strongly pollen limited and have very low reproductive success relative to controls.

Thus the temporal service signal is retained:

`late flowering -> low visitation / pollen limitation -> low fruit and seed set`.

This link is classified as **preserved**.

## Snow-removal experiment gives the same warning

Across years, snow removal advances flowering by about 10 d, but its final reproductive effect depends strongly on frost.

- frost-damaged advanced plants reproduce poorly even when pollen is not limiting;
- advanced plants that escape frost can receive higher visitation and reproduce better than controls.

The full study therefore shows that flowering timing does not have one invariant pollination-to-fitness mapping.

## Why this matters for temporal-information preservation

IWE024 is a prospective direct timing manipulation that yields **mixed retention inside one programme**.

It therefore guards against interpreting the current high preservation rate of prospective timing references as a deterministic rule.

Prospective timing can be biologically clear and still be overwritten by another downstream environmental filter.
