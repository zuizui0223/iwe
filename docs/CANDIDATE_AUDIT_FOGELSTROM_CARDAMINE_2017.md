# Candidate audit — Cardamine pratensis × Anthocharis cardamines experimental synchrony

Date: 2026-09-28  
Study ID: `IWE034`  
Source: Fogelström et al. (2017), *Ecology* 98:703–711  
DOI: `10.1002/ecy.1676`  
Decision: **context-only; direct relative-phenology manipulation but no final plant reproductive endpoint**

## Why this source entered the screen

The study experimentally manipulated the phenology of the butterfly herbivore *Anthocharis cardamines* relative to genetically identical populations of its host plant *Cardamine pratensis*.

It therefore has an unusually strong causal timing architecture:

- plant flowering phenology is controlled/standardized across genetically identical host populations;
- herbivore flight timing is manipulated relative to host flowering;
- butterfly oviposition preference and attack probability are measured;
- the experiment shows that relative phenology changes which host flowering phenotypes experience attack.

## Why it does not enter Tier A

The IWE strict estimand is the effect of partner synchrony on **plant reproductive or lifetime-fitness performance**.

The reported endpoint in this experiment is herbivore preference/attack-mediated selection on flowering phenology. The accessible article and supporting-information metadata do not provide a source-linked final plant outcome such as:

- mature seed production;
- viable seed set;
- fruit set after herbivory;
- realized fecundity; or
- lifetime plant fitness.

An attack probability or herbivore preference function is an interaction/mechanism outcome, not plant reproductive performance.

## Relationship to IWE032

IWE034 involves the same broad *Cardamine–Anthocharis* interaction family as IWE032 (Davies & Saccheri 2024) but is not used to fill IWE032's missing 2012–2014 female timing object.

The studies use different programmes/designs, and IWE034's manipulation cannot be back-projected as the Dibbinsdale female flight window required by IWE032.

## Interpretation

IWE034 is a strong mechanism paper showing that relative phenology alters antagonist attack and phenotypic selection. It remains outside the strict plant-fitness meta-analysis because the final fitness surface is missing, not because its synchrony design is weak.
