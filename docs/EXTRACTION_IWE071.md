# IWE071 extraction receipt — Carduus thoermeri × Rhinocyllus conicus

Source: McCarty MK, Lamp WO. 1982. *Effect of a Weevil, Rhinocyllus conicus, on Musk Thistle (Carduus thoermeri) Seed Production*. Weed Science 30:136–140. DOI `10.1017/S0043174500062202` (historical JSTOR DOI `10.2307/4043766`).

Status: **unresolved strict-H1 candidate; table-level recovery required**.

## Why the design is unusually close to strict H1

The same field study sampled seed heads just after flowering at **four successive weekly intervals** in each of two years (1978 and 1979) and recorded:

- flowering cohort/week;
- numbers of `Rhinocyllus conicus` per head;
- final numbers of fully developed seeds per head;
- an uninfested comparison site.

The accessible source summaries report a strong within-season decline in weevil exposure:

- week 1: 6.7 weevils/head in 1978 and 28.0/head in 1979;
- week 4: less than 1 weevil/head in both years.

They also report final reproductive consequences:

- infested sites produced 28% fewer fully developed seeds than the non-infested site in 1978;
- the reduction was 78% in 1979;
- fully developed seeds had 96–99% germination and germination did not vary with bloom date;
- many heads flowered in weeks 3–4, allowing substantial viable-seed production after weevil incidence had declined.

Thus the study contains both the **partner-interaction timing** and **final viable-seed production** components in the same seasonal design.

## Why no meta-analysis row is entered yet

The publicly accessible abstract does not expose the original week × site table of:

- mean seeds/head;
- uncertainty or raw head-level values;
- number of heads sampled per site/week;
- corresponding weevil incidence for all four weekly cohorts.

The overall annual reduction relative to the uninfested site cannot be converted into a synchrony effect because it does not identify the within-season timing contrast.

Likewise, using only week-1 and week-4 weevil incidence without matched week-specific seed output would mix two different estimands.

## Quantitative closure route

If the original table/full text can be recovered, predeclare the following extraction before looking at signs:

1. treat weekly flowering cohort as the plant temporal axis;
2. use measured weevils/head as the realized antagonist-activity exposure;
3. use fully developed seeds/head as the final reproductive outcome;
4. estimate the within-year association between realized weevil exposure and final seed production;
5. orient the effect as greater synchrony/antagonist exposure -> plant reproductive performance under the standard antagonist sign convention;
6. retain 1978 and 1979 under one dependence/programme ID.

If uncertainty is available only for weekly group means, use a group-level effect family appropriate to the reported sampling distribution rather than inventing individual-level sample sizes.
