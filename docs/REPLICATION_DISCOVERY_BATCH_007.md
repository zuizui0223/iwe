# Antagonist replication discovery batch 007

Date: 2026-09-27  
Target: second independent `antagonist` SMD cluster  
Decision: no new strict-H1 cluster; one new P2 same-programme completion route

## Purpose

The three active antagonist P1 routes are now narrow archive/data blockers rather than broad discovery problems:

1. Wen et al. 2024 *Parnassia* — fate-level data for beetle-destroyed peduncles;
2. James 1998 yucca cheater — timing-stratified plant-level final-seed means/variance;
3. Jordano *Astragalus–Tomares* — correct-unit plant/shoot reproductive variance.

Batch 007 therefore searched for a fully public or near-public fourth programme with the four frozen ingredients:

- independent focal-season antagonist activity;
- a plant timing contrast ordered against that activity window;
- final realized plant reproduction;
- SMD-compatible variance at the correct biological unit.

It also rechecked two previously tempting systems whose blocker classification needed tightening.

## 1. Parnassia wightiana × florivorous beetles — access recheck

The article continues to cite Dryad DOI `10.5061/dryad.b2rbnzsqg` and a reviewer/share route for the full dataset.

The public-access check was repeated on 2026-09-27. The exact DOI still resolves in search primarily to the article/Data Availability statement rather than an indexed retrievable Dryad dataset. No public fate table was recovered.

Decision: unchanged P1 `blocked_final_surface`.

The strict early/middle/late timing window remains strong. The missing object is still the fate-level record for all initially marked flowers so beetle-caused peduncle loss can be included in net reproduction.

## 2. Baptisia australis × Epicauta fabricii — timing gate passes, focal-effect gate fails

Source anchor: Evans et al. 1989, DOI `10.1007/BF00377159`, with the companion blister-beetle aggregation study by Evans (1990).

### What the recheck changes

The earlier ledger treated Baptisia as lacking a single focal antagonist window. That was too pessimistic.

The programme directly recorded adult *Epicauta fabricii* aggregations. The blister beetles arrive relatively late in the flowering season, creating a biologically interpretable opportunity for early flowers to escape their pressure. Therefore the focal *Epicauta* timing gate can be treated as measured.

### Why the programme still cannot enter strict H1

The seasonal plant-fitness contrast is not an isolated *Epicauta* effect.

Across the same flowering season, final reproductive success is simultaneously affected by:

- *Grapholitha* seed/flower consumers;
- *Tychius* weevils;
- other chewing insects;
- late-season killing frosts.

Thus early-versus-late seed production changes multiple selective agents at once. The final response cannot be attributed to greater versus lower synchrony with *Epicauta* alone.

Decision: change from `rejected_timing` to `rejected_other`, DROP.

This distinction matters: the study is not rejected because antagonist phenology was unmeasured; it is rejected because the focal synchrony effect is inseparable from other seasonally structured causes on the final-fitness surface.

## 3. Ipomopsis aggregata × Hylemya — excellent cohort/final-seed data, wrong partner window

Source: Price et al. 2021, DOI `10.1086/713440`.  
Data: Dryad DOI `10.6086/D18D7B`.

The programme repeatedly censused flowers and elongated buds, marked phenological cohorts, recorded *Hylemya* eggs, and followed reproduction to final seed production. The data architecture is therefore unusually attractive.

However, the apparent antagonist window is defined from eggs received by host flowers. Egg receipt is a realized plant–fly interaction, not an independent census of adult fly availability. It already depends on host availability and oviposition choice.

Under the frozen IWE partner-window rule, the egg series cannot be recycled as the independent antagonist activity curve.

Decision: `rejected_timing`, DROP.

## 4. Eureka 2001 squarrose knapweed × Larinus/Urophora — new P2 route

System: *Centaurea virgata* ssp. *squarrosa* with seed-feeding *Larinus minutus* and *Urophora quadrifasciata*.  
Public synthesis: Evans 2016, DOI `10.1007/s13355-016-0401-z`.

### What passes

At Eureka, Utah in 2001:

- adult *Larinus* and *Urophora* abundance was measured independently by 100 sweep-net sweeps each week from late spring through summer;
- after flowering began, up to 200 newly open flowers were marked each week with cohort-specific strings;
- those marked flowers were collected 4–6 weeks later as mature seed heads and dissected;
- cohort infestation changes through the season in the same programme and can be interpreted relative to the independently measured adult activity curves.

This is stronger timing architecture than candidates that infer predator availability from eggs, infestation or damage.

### Current blocker

The public synthesis exposes the timing and cohort infestation results, but not a variance-bearing final viable-seed/seed-production surface for the same tagged weekly cohorts.

Therefore infestation percentage cannot be used as the plant reproductive endpoint.

Companion squarrose-knapweed studies and annual reports do contain seed-production or seed-destruction quantities, but those values are not automatically interchangeable with the 2001 Eureka cohort experiment. IWE will not splice California, other Utah sites, or other years into the focal timing programme.

Decision: add `ANT002_KNAPWEED_LARINUS_2001` as P2 `blocked_final_surface`.

## Exact unlock for the knapweed route

The candidate advances if the 2001 Eureka programme yields a source-backed table with:

- weekly flower cohort/date;
- the same marked mature seed-head identity or cohort;
- final viable seed number or another post-predation seed-production endpoint;
- sample size and variance at a defensible biological unit.

A unit-level marked-head dataset would be sufficient if the independent unit and clustering can be reconstructed without pseudoreplication.

Antagonist #2 remains unevaluable, but the search state is narrower:

- P1: Parnassia fate table;
- P1: James yucca-cheater plant-level final-seed summaries;
- P1: Tomares correct-unit variance;
- P2: Cirsium–Rhinocyllus same-unit synchrony/final-seed table;

Batch 007 therefore produces one genuine new completion route while closing two attractive false shortcuts.
