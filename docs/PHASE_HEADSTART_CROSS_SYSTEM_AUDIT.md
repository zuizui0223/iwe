# Cross-system phase-head-start audit

Date: 2026-10-03  
Status: descriptive cross-system comparison; no pooled effect size and no universal threshold claim.

## Question

The stage-specific model predicts that a plant can reduce delayed consumer cost when its reproductive tissue gains enough developmental head start before the damaging stage arrives.

This audit asks whether the **direction** of a response-independent phase coordinate agrees with downstream biological fate across the systems where the source permits such a comparison.

The quantities below are not commensurate effect sizes and are never averaged.

## Silene stellata–Hadena ectypa

Source programme: Kula 2012, Mountain Lake Biological Station.

Define:

`phase safety margin = flower-to-first-larva delay - mean fruit maturation time`.

| Year | Flower -> first larva | Fruit maturation | Phase margin | Synchrony -> predation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 2008 | 10 d | 21.3 d | **-11.3 d** | positive |
| 2009 | 17 d | 16.7 d | **+0.3 d** | negative |

The margin crosses zero in the same year that flowering × oviposition synchrony changes from predicting more predation to predicting less predation.

This is a two-year mechanistic contrast, not a fitted threshold.

## Cardamine pratensis–Anthocharis cardamines

The source classifies eggs laid within **7 days** of first flowering as active: those eggs are early enough to yield damaging later larvae. Eggs laid at ≥8 days are inactive for that future-damage definition.

At the ecotype level:

| Ecotype | Mean flowering -> egg interval | Descriptive margin from 7-d active boundary | Dehisce before larval completion |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| early | 11.50 ± 1.44 d | **+4.50 d** | **34%** |
| late | 5.72 ± 0.53 d | **-1.28 d** | **4%** |

The phase direction agrees with final attacked-plant fate: the ecotype with a positive mean head-start margin is much more often able to dehisce before larval completion.

However, the timing means and final-instar fate percentages are ecotype-level summaries and are **not assumed to represent the same individual subset**. This is descriptive alignment, not an individual-level effect estimate.

The fate distribution also includes a wholly consumed category:

- early ecotype: 41%;
- late ecotype: 58%;

with overall fate difference chi-square(df=2) = 11.95, p = 0.003.

## Independent null: Yucca kanabensis cheater moth

James 1998 shows why an upstream phase/timing effect need not persist.

Flowering timing and fruit age strongly affect oviposition exposure, yet flowering date, isolation and fruit number do not predict realized cheater larval number (n = 359; model R² = 0.000).

The thesis identifies host stage and interspecific competition with pollinator larvae as important downstream filters.

Thus a timing signal can disappear between exposure and consumer realization.

## Independent null: experimental developmental race

Posledovich 2015 manipulates host phenological state at oviposition and developmental temperature.

Those variables strongly affect *A. cardamines* larval performance, but the probability that the first host outgrows the larva and forms mature seedpods depends only on host species identity.

A biologically correct stage coordinate therefore need not predict the chosen final plant endpoint.

## Long-term null: Lathyrus vernus–Bruchus atomarius

Across 21 years, spring temperature changes which flowering phenologies experience seed predation.

Yet the yearly flowering-date × seed-predation covariance does not explain flowering-time selection on intact-seed fitness:

- estimate = **-0.033**;
- 95% CI = **-0.101 to +0.037**.

Again, a moving timing-to-attack relation need not propagate to plant selection.

## What survives this audit

The evidence supports a restricted statement:

> developmental head start is a plausible and independently recurring mechanism that can alter whether exposure becomes damaging, but its predictive value must be demonstrated at the downstream endpoint rather than assumed.

Silene and Cardamine provide positive directional examples at different levels of evidence. James, Posledovich and long-term Lathyrus provide mandatory nulls at later stages of the chain.

## What is not claimed

This audit does not claim:

- a universal zero-day threshold;
- one common phase metric across taxa;
- a pooled phase effect;
- independent final-fitness replication of the Kula result;
- that ecotype-level Cardamine means imply an individual-level phase effect.

The next confirmatory target is an independent programme with a source-defined phase coordinate and final post-cost plant reproduction measured at compatible units.
