# Senita 2011 mixed-system component-linkage closure

Date: 2026-10-02  
Candidate: `MIX002_SENITA_2011`  
System: *Lophocereus schottii × Upiga virescens*  
Source DOI: `10.1111/j.1600-0706.2010.18958.x`  
Decision: **DROP from strict mixed-completion queue; timing and final reproduction occur in separate experimental components.**

## Why this looked promising

The senita cactus–senita moth system is a genuine pollinating seed-consumer mutualism. The 2011 programme explicitly contains seasonal variation in moth abundance/oviposition and also follows moth-exposed flowers to ripe fruit.

At first glance this resembles the desired mixed landscape architecture.

## Component A — early season, high moth density

During the early flowering season, when senita moth density was high, the study censused approximately:

- 977 flowers;
- 31 plants;
- over two nights after the main nocturnal moth-activity period.

These flowers were used to quantify pollination/oviposition patterns under high moth density.

This component does **not** carry those early high-moth flowers through to a matched ripe-fruit or post-larval final reproductive endpoint.

## Component B — later season, intermediate moth abundance

Later in the flowering season, when moth abundance was intermediate, the study labelled:

- 716 egg-bearing flowers;
- 27 plants.

Those flowers were followed every two days through fruit set, fruit survival, ripe-fruit production and larval survival in the ant/extrafloral-nectary experiment.

Thus the later component has a final ripe-fruit endpoint.

## Why the components cannot be combined

The high-moth early observations and the later ripe-fruit observations are not two timing groups of one common experimental cohort.

Constructing a high-versus-intermediate seasonal effect would require splicing:

- partner-activity/pollination information from the early experiment; and
- final fruit survival from the later ant/EFN experiment.

That would manufacture a timing-to-final-fitness contrast that the source did not follow at a common biological unit.

The existence of both measurements somewhere in one publication is not sufficient.

## Consequence

The candidate is no longer best described as `blocked_final_surface`.

The public design itself demonstrates that the missing linkage is structural: there is no same-component early-high versus later-intermediate moth window carried to matched post-cost final reproduction.

Therefore:

- strict mixed-H1 status: rejected;
- landscape role: mechanism/context only;
- completion priority: DROP;
- no author/archive retrieval is warranted merely to join two source-defined experiments post hoc.

## General lesson

For mixed pollinating seed predators, all four quantities must be linked within the same biological comparison:

1. plant timing;
2. independently characterized partner activity;
3. benefit/cost exposure;
4. post-cost plant reproduction.

A publication containing all four somewhere in the paper still fails if they belong to incompatible experimental components.
