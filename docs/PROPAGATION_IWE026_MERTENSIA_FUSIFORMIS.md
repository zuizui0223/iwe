# IWE026 Forrest & Thomson 2010 — resource allocation and frost buffer flowering-time effects

Date: 2026-10-07  
Study: *Mertensia fusiformis*  
DOI: `10.3732/ajb.0900083`  
Propagation role: seasonal flowering-time signal buffered before total seed fitness.

## Source pattern

The study follows flowering phenology and reproduction over two contrasting years.

Selection favors early-flowering individuals only in 2008, not in the earlier 2007 season.

Within plants:

- early flowers produce more and heavier seeds in 2008;
- the same early-vs-late advantage is absent in 2007;
- late flowers can produce as many seeds as early flowers when fertilization of early ovules is prevented.

This demonstrates flexible resource allocation among reproductive units.

Evidence for pollen limitation is weak and changes direction between years.

Poor reproduction in 2007 is attributed more to frost damage than to pollinator shortage.

## Propagation state

The source identifies compensatory biology that prevents one stable flowering-time effect from propagating to total seed production.

The link is classified as **buffered**:

`seasonal flowering position -> variable pollination/frost/resource effects -> flexible allocation and year-dependent total seed fitness`.

The compensation is source demonstrated rather than inferred from a null final test.

## Claim boundary

No independently measured partner-activity window is available for the focal seasonal relation, so the upstream reference is `seasonal_position_only`.

The result does not imply absence of phenological selection; it shows that the downstream reproductive consequence is buffered and context dependent.
