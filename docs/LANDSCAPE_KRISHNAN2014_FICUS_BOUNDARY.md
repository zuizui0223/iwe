# Ficus racemosa seasonal mixed-system boundary

Date: 2026-10-02  
Study: Krishnan et al. 2014, *Ficus racemosa* brood-site pollination mutualism  
PMID: `25521512`  
Landscape role: mixed-system boundary showing that nursery size and climate can dominate timing effects.

## Design

Sixteen *Ficus racemosa* trees were followed for 20 months from November 2008 to August 2010.

Within each tree, 20 fig bunches were marked and syconium developmental stages were recorded every 2–3 days. The study observed 94 reproductive episodes distributed across four climate-defined seasons.

Syconia used for plant-trait measurements were dissected to count:

- seeds;
- pollinating fig-wasp offspring;
- non-pollinating parasite offspring.

Thus plant seed production and pollinator reproduction were quantified within the same nursery structures.

## Seasonal result

Summer was approximately 3–5 C warmer than the cooler seasons.

Relative to cooler seasons:

- pollinating fig-wasp reproduction fell by roughly two-fold;
- seed production per syconium increased by roughly two-fold.

The authors interpret much of this seasonal shift through smaller summer syconia and reduced wasp survival/oviposition under hotter conditions.

## Within-tree asynchrony is not a simple benefit axis

Within-tree reproductive asynchrony itself was broadly similar among seasons, but its biological effect changed with season.

For pollinator progeny, within-tree asynchrony had:

- a significant positive effect in winter;
- no effect in the hot-day/cold-night season;
- a significant negative effect in summer;
- a weak non-significant negative effect in the wet season.

Asynchrony therefore does not map to one invariant mixed-system fitness effect.

## Why this is a boundary for IWE

This system contains a pollinator whose offspring develop in host reproductive tissue, so seed and pollinator production share the same nursery resource.

Yet the observed seed-versus-wasp balance changes strongly with climate and syconium size even though within-tree phenology is not itself changing strongly across seasons.

The important boundary is:

> mixed-system net outcome can shift because environmental conditions change nursery capacity and partner reproduction, even without a cleaner or larger plant–partner timing mismatch.

Therefore mixed landscapes should not be reduced to synchrony alone.

## Claim boundary

The study does not provide a partner-relative timing exposure for IWE:

- within-tree asynchrony is a host internal phenology metric;
- adult pollinator availability is not the exposure used to order plant timing groups;
- season is strongly confounded with temperature, humidity and nursery size.

This component is therefore registered as `boundary_evidence`, not as a strict or realized interaction-window effect.

It is deliberately excluded from counts of programmes supporting the window-relative hypothesis.
