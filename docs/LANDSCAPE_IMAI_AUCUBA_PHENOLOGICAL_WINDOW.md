# Imai & Ohsaki — a directly manipulated phenological window reaches final reproductive fate

Date: 2026-10-03  
System: *Aucuba japonica × Asphondylia aucubae*  
Core sources: Imai & Ohsaki 2006; Imai & Ohsaki 2009  
DOIs: `10.1603/0046-225X-35.4.1109`, `10.1603/022.038.0213`  
Landscape role: independent antagonist experiment linking adult-season timing, host developmental filtering, and final seed-producing versus non-seed-producing fruit fate.

## Why this system is unusually informative

The specialist midge attacks young *Aucuba* fruits.

Successful gall induction destroys the embryo sac, so a completely galled fruit cannot produce a seed. The plant therefore has a direct reproductive consequence from whether an oviposition event falls inside the susceptible host window.

The source separates:

1. **adult midge emergence**;
2. **experimental oviposition date within the adult season**;
3. **host fruit developmental state**;
4. **gall-induction / final fruit fate** roughly five months later.

This is closer to the stage-specific IWE target than a simple calendar flowering-date association.

## Adult window

Adult emergence was monitored directly from previous-year galls.

In 1999, the entire adult emergence season lasted nearly 20 days with a clear peak.

In the 2005 experiment, adult emergence was again monitored on the focal shrubs. By 15 June, **345 of 428 adults (83%)** had emerged.

Thus the adult activity window is independently observed rather than inferred from damage.

## Experimental timing manipulation

Experimental infructescences were bagged before the natural adult season.

Field-collected females that were actively ovipositing were released individually into cages for one day at different dates from **3 to 23 June**, at 2–7 day intervals.

Across three shrubs, 21 females were used.

This directly manipulates encounter/oviposition timing relative to fruit development while excluding uncontrolled natural oviposition.

## Host developmental filter

The usable host window is bounded by two different tissues.

### Opening

Very early fruits are protected by a hard endocarp that covers potential oviposition sites.

The 2009 follow-up experimentally shows that earliest-emerging females attacking the earliest fruit stages are often unable to oviposit until cracks develop in this mechanical barrier.

### Closing

Later in fruit development, the integument—the reactive tissue needed to form larval chambers—rapidly degenerates as the embryo sac expands.

The 2006 experiment shows that late oviposition therefore has much lower probability of producing a complete gall.

## Final reproductive-fate result

About five months after treatment, remaining fruits were collected and classified.

Before 15 June:

- complete gall induction succeeded in **55/68 = 80.9%** of oviposited fruits.

After 15 June:

- complete gall induction succeeded in only **3/34 = 8.8%**.

The release-date effect was strong:

- slope = **-0.24 ± 0.06 SE**;
- likelihood-ratio chi-square = **28.65**;
- **p < 0.00001**.

Complete galls had more larval chambers than incomplete galls:

- complete: **2.86 ± 1.46 SD**;
- incomplete: **1.43 ± 0.77 SD**;
- Welch F = **31.8**, p < 0.0001.

Most importantly for the plant endpoint, complete gall formation causes embryo-sac degeneration and makes seed production impossible. Healthy fruit retain seed-producing potential; incomplete galls develop hardened embryo sacs and do not become successful midge galls.

Thus a shift within the adult season crosses a host developmental boundary and changes the reproductive fate of the fruit.

## Interpretation

This independently supports the core stage-specific principle:

> adult availability alone is insufficient; the consequence of the same adult interaction depends on whether host tissue is inside its susceptible developmental window.

The system is particularly useful because both boundaries of the window have identified plant mechanisms:

- hard endocarp before the window;
- loss of reactive integument after the window.

## Why this is not yet the full confirmatory comparison

The experiment demonstrates causal timing dependence and final reproductive fate, but it does not fit a paired predictive comparison of:

1. adult/calendar timing alone; versus
2. an independently parameterized tissue-stage coordinate

on the same experimental fruits.

The 1999 host-tissue series and 2005 timing experiment establish the mechanism, and the 2009 experiment establishes the early mechanical boundary, but the published summaries do not provide one unit-level dataset for a direct model-performance comparison.

Accordingly:

- landscape status: `experimental_timing_evidence`;
- phase-gate status: `host_sensitivity_final`;
- confirmatory-ready: **no**.
