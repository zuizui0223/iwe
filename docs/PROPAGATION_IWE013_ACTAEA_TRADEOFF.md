# IWE013 Ehrlén et al. 2015 — seed predation can reverse the intrinsic early-flower fitness advantage

Date: 2026-10-07  
Study: *Actaea spicata*  
DOI: `10.1890/14-1860.1`  
Propagation role: seasonal offspring-quality advantage transformed by seed-predator escape.

## Source pattern

Within plants, early terminal flowers produce higher-quality offspring than late basal flowers.

For intact seeds, estimated reproductive value of early flowers is about **two times** that of late flowers.

The antagonist acts in the opposite direction: late fruits are much more likely to escape pre-dispersal seed predation.

The source estimates that early and late reproductive values balance at about **63% seed predation**.

Across 15 natural populations, stronger mean seed predation is associated with stronger selection for allocation to late flowers.

## Propagation state

The link is classified as **sign_reversed** at the antagonist-processing step:

`intrinsic early offspring-fitness advantage -> stronger early seed predation -> increasing advantage of late allocation as predation rises`.

The source therefore demonstrates that a seasonal fitness ordering can be reversed by the downstream antagonist stage.

## Claim boundary

The focal predator window is not measured contemporaneously as an independent adult-activity series, and the early/late flowers are repeated reproductive units within maternal plants.

This is a propagation result on a seasonal coordinate, not strict H1.
