# Measurement note — Carduus × Rhinocyllus

Date: 2026-09-18

The Carduus–Rhinocyllus literature is useful for IWE because it contains examples of all three measurement architectures.

## 1. Direct synchrony, missing final fitness

Surles & Kok (1977) directly compared synchronization of weevil oviposition with two Carduus hosts, but the study is primarily an oviposition/synchronization study rather than a final seed-fitness analysis.

## 2. Final seed production and seasonal exposure in the same study, quantitative table not yet recovered

McCarty & Lamp (1982; IWE071) sampled four weekly flowering cohorts and recorded both weevil incidence and fully developed seeds/head. This is the strongest recovery target in the cluster.

Goeden & Ricker (1985; IWE072) followed a host flowering season that extended well beyond the end of weevil oviposition and showed that most surviving seeds were produced in this temporal refuge, but accessible summaries lack the variance structure needed for a meta effect.

## 3. Direct abundance synchrony and damage, missing final seed output

A recent Pennsylvania urban–rural study (IWE073; DOI `10.1007/s10530-024-03301-w`) directly measured both plant and adult-weevil phenology and two abundance-based synchrony metrics. Those synchrony metrics did not vary with temperature, while seasonal oviposition/damage patterns did.

This is a particularly important caution for IWE:

> adult presence synchrony is not necessarily interaction synchrony.

The authors explicitly note that adult presence on thistles does not guarantee active oviposition. Where possible, IWE should therefore prefer egg/oviposition timing over adult abundance when the antagonist's reproductive attack is the biological mechanism.

## Implication

The cluster reinforces the evidence-architecture diagnosis while also supplying plausible strict recovery targets. It does not justify relaxing the strict endpoint or substituting attack rate for final plant reproduction.
