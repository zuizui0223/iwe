# Wise 2015 — experimentally shifting adult wheat-midge exposure changes final seed damage

Date: 2026-10-06  
Study: Wise, Fox & Smith 2015, spring wheat × orange wheat blossom midge (*Sitodiplosis mosellana*)  
DOI: `10.4039/tce.2014.75`  
Landscape role: independent direct-interaction-timing evidence linking host developmental stage at adult exposure to final seed damage/yield loss.

## Why this is useful

Unlike most observational antagonist studies, the programme does not infer timing from egg receipt or damage.

Adult wheat midges are presented to wheat spikes at different times from spike emergence through anthesis. The experiment therefore changes **when the adult interaction occurs relative to host reproductive development**.

The final endpoint is seed damage / yield loss after larval feeding.

## Source-backed timing result

The source abstract reports two consistent final-outcome patterns.

1. Across all resistant cultivars, seed damage declines as plants are exposed to adult midge later from spike emergence toward anthesis.
2. In susceptible cultivars, spikes exposed **two or more days after emergence** have lower yield losses than spikes exposed at the time of emergence.

Thus an experimentally shifted adult-exposure window is preserved to a final reproductive cost.

## Host filtering

The experiment also shows that host genotype changes conversion of exposure into downstream cost.

- Sm1-resistant cultivars have significantly lower yield loss than susceptible cultivars.
- The Shaw resistant cultivar has no third-instar larvae on seeds in either field or laboratory tests.

This separates two causal layers:

`adult exposure timing -> host susceptibility / larval development -> final seed damage`.

The timing signal is positive evidence, while resistance is a pre-final filter that changes its magnitude.

## Relation to Wu 2015

Wu 2015 independently monitors adult midge activity in the field, identifies ear emergence as the susceptible stage by bagging, and relates adult–ear-emergence overlap to yield loss across >400 cultivars.

Wise 2015 supplies a complementary experimental manipulation:

- Wu: natural adult timing × cultivar host-stage timing;
- Wise: controlled adult exposure at different host reproductive stages.

The two programmes are independent and must retain separate dependence IDs.

## Claim boundary

This study does not provide a paired model comparison showing that a stage-specific coordinate predicts final loss better than a simpler calendar or adult-only coordinate.

The currently public abstract also does not expose all timing-group means/variances needed for a new common effect size.

It is therefore registered as:

- `experimental_timing_evidence` in the landscape registry;
- `host_sensitivity_final` in the phase-alignment gate;
- `preserved` in the temporal signal-propagation ledger.

It strengthens the positive side of the stage-specific model without closing the stricter predictive-superiority gate.
