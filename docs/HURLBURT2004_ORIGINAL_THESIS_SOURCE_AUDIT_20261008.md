## Important partial closure: true marked-flower to fruit-fate lineage exists

**Physical PDF page 85, printed thesis page 73**, original Methods:
individual flowers on marked inflorescences were visited **2–7 times per
week**. For each marked flower, the author recorded the opening date and
later **fate** (abscised, visited, retained or eaten); a unique flower
number written on a tepal was **re-written onto the enlarging ovary** if
the flower became a fruit. Thus source Methods establish a real
flower-identity lineage, rather than just co-occurrence of unrelated
flower and fruit samples.

In the same original paragraph, adult female `Tegeticula yuccasella`
density was separately observed from approximately **100 fresh flowers**
at each main site **2–3 times per week**. As previously noted, adult
detectability is conditioned on fresh-host-flower survey availability:
these are **not an unconditional flight/emergence calendar**, and
unobserved adult days are not known zero-abundance days.

**Physical PDF page 86, printed page 74**, original *Fruit
Dissection* Methods: every **marked** flower was collected either
when it abscised or at **six weeks post-pollination** when retained;
collected units were dissected for oviposition tracks. Mature
fruits were also dissected for moth larvae per locule/fruit.
Consequently a **flower opening → marked flower fate / fruit retention**
link was present in the original study design. This revises the
earlier blanket claim that the mature-fruit *identity* bridge itself
was unknown.

**Physical PDF page 87, printed page 75**, the thesis states that
visitation, fruiting and retention were calculated within inflorescences
and averaged for sites/years. It also says multiple inflorescences
from one clone were *sometimes treated as independent samples*.
Any new IWE effect must reinstate original clone-level dependence
where the same clone appears more than once.

## The remaining gap is narrower, but still decisive

The same Methods **do not publish** a table with:

- every marked flower's opening date;
- its matched clone/inflorescence/flower identity;
- the contemporaneous female moth census dates/effort;
- its own **post-larval intact viable-seed number** and sampling variance.

The Chapter 3 dissected marked units were explicitly scored for
oviposition and moth larvae, but this does **not** establish that
viable seed counts were reported or retained by those same
marked-flower/date IDs. The Methods mention a marked-versus-unmarked
viable-seed comparison as **unpublished data**, which is not a
recoverable source row table. Chapter 4 (physical PDF pp.149–155)
does contain actual viable-seed measurements **in separately
hand-pollinated pollen-source experiments**. Those do not restore
a natural adult-activity versus flowering timing-to-seed surface
without an explicit same-unit join.

Therefore update the unlock from “do flower fate IDs exist?” to:
**Can the marked-flower/date IDs be joined to independently
measured adult activity and to terminal intact-seed outcomes
at compatible units?**

## A newly recovered published temporal negative/boundary

**Physical PDF page 95, printed 83**, Chapter 3 states that the
calendar distribution of observed fruiting was generally proportional
to the distribution of flowers available through a season; the
population-level time pattern of oviposition-defined visitation
also did not show a significant departure from fruiting.

**Table 3.10, physical PDF page 127, printed 115**, provides the
actual runs-test statistics for nine site-years (Onefour 1999–2002;
Loma 2000–2002; Fort Benton 2001–2002). Exact printed values are
preserved in
`data/source_reconstructions/hurlburt2004_original_thesis_table3_10_runs.csv`.

- Across 9 site-years, flowering-versus-fruiting runs tests all have
  P > 0.05 (range **0.216–1.000**).
- Across those same 9 site-years, visitation-versus-fruiting runs
  tests also all have P > 0.05 (range **0.171–1.000**).
- For **Onefour** 1999–2002, flowering-versus-fruiting
  P=0.244, 0.579, 0.598, 0.216.

These are **published non-rejections for pattern correspondence**,
**not** evidence of effect-size equivalence, true zero timing selection,
or an absence of host-stage effects. Runs tests use time-series
structure, not a recoverable plant-level matched final-seed response;
some site-year `n` are as small as five or six. They cannot be
relabeled a negative strict-H1 SMD or pooled as independent
fitness contrasts.

## Ecological implication and stop rule

The original Hurlburt programme contains a richer biological
chain than previously recoverable from COSEWIC alone:
individual marked flowering dates → retained/aborted flowers and
within-flower moth activity. The published *population* temporal
comparison, however, does not show a detectable temporal separation
of fruiting from flowering. This is an independent important boundary
for claims of a universal shape-shift between activity and fruit output.

Still **not verified**:
`same unit/date/partner activity -> final intact seed number/variance`.
Strict mixed-H1 cluster contribution remains **zero**.
Do not build a Hedges g from year totals, runs Z statistics,
unmatched Chapter 4 pollen treatments, or apparently constant
fruit/inflorescence rates.
