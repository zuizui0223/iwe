# Després 2004 — stage role differences do not map directly onto final seed output

Date: 2026-10-07  
Study: Després 2004, *Trollius europaeus × Chiastocheta* spp.  
DOI: `10.1111/j.1365-2656.2004.00785.x`  
Landscape role: independent mixed-system boundary showing that distinct oviposition-stage roles can be buffered before final plant seed output.

## Design

A total of 169 young *Trollius europaeus* fruits were selected at the end of flowering in one French Alpine population and bagged according to their egg composition.

The source distinguishes:

- **early** *Chiastocheta* — especially *C. rotundiventris*, ovipositing in young flowers;
- **late** species — ovipositing later in the flowering / young-fruit sequence.

Fruit categories included:

1. one early egg;
2. two early eggs;
3. late eggs only;
4. one early egg plus late eggs;
5. two early eggs plus late eggs.

Fruits were followed through larval development and collected at first larval exit / carpel dehiscence. Seed production before predation and intact seeds remaining after predation were measured on the same fruits.

## Stage roles differ strongly

The source explicitly interprets the species differently.

Early flies visit young, often unpollinated flowers and provide substantial pollination while usually depositing only one egg.

At the latest extreme, *C. dentifera* oviposits on maturing fruits and contributes essentially no new pollination through that female visit.

Thus early and late species occupy different adult-service positions in flower development.

## Final seed output is much less different than the stage roles

Despite those strong stage-role differences, source summary values for the fruits associated with early and late larvae are similar at the mature-seed endpoint:

- seed number before predation: **304.9 ± 128.6** for early vs **340.7 ± 114.0** for late;
- intact seed number after predation: **140.4 ± 81.3** for early vs **146.6 ± 76.6** for late;
- seeds eaten per larva: **46.4 ± 32.8** for early vs **33.0 ± 19.5** for late.

The study does not present these rows as a causal early-versus-late plant-fitness contrast, and the categories are not independent timing treatments.

The important observation is therefore qualitative and structural:

> large differences in adult interaction stage and pollination contribution can be strongly buffered before the final intact-seed endpoint.

## Why buffering occurs

Three mechanisms intervene between adult stage timing and final plant reproduction.

1. **Larval installation differs:** early larval installation success is higher than late (53.8% vs 40.4%).
2. **Competition is density dependent:** seed consumption per larva falls as larval density increases.
3. **Resource size matters:** larger fruits host more eggs and lose more seeds, but also release more undamaged seeds.

Even in this unusually heavily parasitized sample, no fruit lost all of its seeds; average seed loss was about 55%.

## Consequence for the stage-specific model

This is a boundary against a simple mapping:

`earlier/later interaction stage -> different adult role -> proportional difference in final plant fitness`.

Stage identity is biologically meaningful, but final reproduction can be buffered by density dependence, larval competition and host resource size.

The confirmatory IWE claim must therefore remain predictive:

> does a stage-specific coordinate explain final plant fitness better than calendar/adult-only timing after these downstream nonlinearities are allowed?

## Claim boundary

This study does not manipulate phase lag and does not estimate a source-defined early-versus-late effect on final plant fitness.

It is registered as `stage_structure_evidence`, not as a supporting final-fitness landscape programme.

The source values are descriptive and must not be converted into an independent-groups SMD.
