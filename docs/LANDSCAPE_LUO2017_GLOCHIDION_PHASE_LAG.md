# Luo 2017 — an annual-scale stage-specific window in Glochidion–Epicephala

Date: 2026-10-02  
Study: Luo et al. 2017, *Glochidion lanceolarium × Epicephala lanceolaria*  
DOI: `10.1086/690623`  
Landscape role: independent mixed-system stage-structure evidence with an extreme adult-service to offspring-cost phase lag.

## Annual interaction sequence

The plant and moth each have one main reproductive cycle per year.

The source directly documents:

- **April–May:** female *E. lanceolaria* actively collect and deposit pollen and oviposit into female flowers;
- **May–December:** developing fruits and moth eggs remain dormant together;
- **January–February:** fruits begin rapid growth while seeds initially remain dormant; moth eggs hatch and larvae enter the carpel chamber;
- **mid/late January–February:** seeds develop rapidly and larvae consume one of the two seeds in an occupied carpel;
- **mid-February–early March:** larvae pupate inside fruits;
- **March–early April:** adults eclose inside mature fruits and are released when fruits dehisce, immediately before/around the next flowering season.

Thus the positive adult service and negative offspring cost are separated by approximately **eight months** of synchronized host-fruit / moth-egg dormancy.

## Why this matters for stage-specific windows

This is an extreme independent replication of the key IWE distinction:

> synchrony with the adult pollinator is not the same temporal variable as exposure to its seed-consuming offspring.

The plant–moth system has evolved a life cycle in which the host itself stores the pollinator's egg through a long dormant interval and then resumes fruit/seed development at the time the larval cost is expressed.

The relevant mixed interaction is therefore intrinsically multi-stage:

1. adult pollination/oviposition;
2. long dormant phase;
3. host fruit reactivation;
4. larval seed consumption;
5. adult release near the next flowering season.

A single adult-overlap score would erase most of this temporal structure.

## Mature seed fate is directly observed

The source reports mature per-flower seed/egg outcomes across four populations.

Examples of mean ± SE values include:

| Population | Eggs laid | Intact seeds | Infested seeds | Inviable seeds |

| Gaobangshan | 2.4 ± 1.8 | 8.4 ± 2.8 | 2.4 ± 1.9 | 3.6 ± 3.2 |
| Qiao Island | 2.6 ± 1.2 | 3.2 ± 1.3 | 2.6 ± 1.2 | 7.2 ± 2.1 |
| SCBG | 2.2 ± 1.8 | 9.9 ± 3.1 | 2.2 ± 1.8 | 1.1 ± 2.0 |
| Wutongshan | 3.5 ± 1.8 | 5.2 ± 2.6 | 3.6 ± 1.9 | 4.8 ± 2.1 |

These data verify that the delayed larval stage reaches mature seed fate in the same biological mutualism.

## Relation to Kula 2012 and Cardamine

The three systems reveal different scales of the same architecture.

- **Silene–Hadena:** a shift of days in flowering/egg-to-larva phase relation reverses the sign of synchrony on predation.
- **Cardamine–Anthocharis:** host-stage filtering shifts and narrows the subset of egg exposure capable of producing later damaging larvae.
- **Glochidion–Epicephala:** adult service and larval seed cost are separated by months of coordinated dormancy.

This makes developmental phase structure a general biological object rather than an idiosyncrasy of one Mountain Lake system.

## Claim boundary

The study does not compare naturally varying high-versus-low phase lags and does not estimate a timing effect on final plant fitness.

It therefore cannot test whether window realignment reduces effect heterogeneity.

The component is registered as `stage_structure_evidence`, not as a supporting landscape programme or strict mixed effect.

Its role is mechanistic generality: it independently demonstrates that a nursery-pollinating seed predator can have adult service and offspring cost windows separated by most of a year.
