# IWE008 Wang et al. 2024 — signed plant–pollinator peak mismatch propagates asymmetrically to seed setting

Date: 2026-10-07  
Study: Wang et al. 2024, Qilian Mountains alpine grassland community  
DOI: `10.1016/j.scitotenv.2024.170458`  
Propagation role: independently monitored signed plant–key-pollinator peak mismatch linked to plant seed setting.

## Design

The study monitors a natural alpine grassland community with 26 sufficiently abundant insect-pollinated plant species.

For focal plant species, the source measures through time:

- flowering abundance;
- insect visitation;
- the peak date of flowering;
- the peak date of the key pollinator;
- individual-level seed setting.

Plant and pollinator peak timing is therefore measured independently of the final seed-setting response.

## Signed mismatch is biologically asymmetric

The source retains the two directions of peak mismatch:

1. **flower peaks earlier** than the key pollinator;
2. **pollinator peaks earlier** than the flower.

The final fitness impact is not symmetric.

The source reports that the “pollinator peaks earlier” pattern has a significantly stronger detrimental effect on plant seed setting than the “flower peaks earlier” pattern.

The asymmetry becomes larger as plant flowering duration becomes shorter.

Thus the temporal relation is not well represented by absolute mismatch alone.

## Propagation adjudication

The source-backed timing coordinate is a signed difference between independently monitored plant and key-pollinator peak dates.

Increasing mismatch is associated with reduced seed-setting success, with the pollinator-earlier side substantially steeper/more detrimental.

The timing signal therefore reaches the final reproductive endpoint and is classified as:

`preserved`.

The asymmetry is retained explicitly in the evidence note and must not be erased by converting the timing variable to an unsigned distance.

## Dependence and scope

The 26 plant species are embedded in one monitored community and share years, sites and pollinator environment.

For the propagation ledger they are therefore represented conservatively by one programme-level link:

`DEP_QILIAN_WANG2021_2022`.

This study is not used as 26 independent replications.

## Why this matters for the pivot

IWE001 already showed that a one-sided mismatch slope can be strong when plants lead pollinators.

IWE008 adds the complementary community result that the two sides of mismatch can differ systematically in fitness consequence.

The general lesson is:

> temporal information can be preserved to final fitness while its magnitude remains direction-dependent.

Signal preservation therefore does not imply symmetry.
