# Fig-system mixed-route closure

Date: 2026-10-02  
Target: independent mixed pollinating-seed-predator timing-to-final-fitness programme  
Decision: **close the current Ficus route as context, not an active strict-completion route.**

## Ficus aurea — Bronstein & Patel 1992

Source: DOI `10.2307/2445195`.

The paper directly quantifies within-tree phenological synchrony and the temporal overlap of fig developmental stages that could allow pollinating wasps to remain on their natal tree.

That is a biologically important timing phenotype, but it is not the IWE strict exposure:

- synchrony is among fig cohorts/stages within the host tree;
- the focal analysis asks whether stages that permit local wasp cycling overlap;
- no source-defined partner-availability contrast is carried to a post-cost seed-production outcome.

The source therefore does not supply a same-unit `partner timing -> net plant reproduction` surface.

## Ficus rubiginosa — Cook & Power 1996

Source: DOI `10.1111/j.1365-2699.1996.tb00010.x`.

This Australian monoecious fig is genuinely asynchronous within trees, and the paper measures both seed and pollinating-wasp production.

However, the source does not turn within-tree asynchrony into a plant-versus-partner timing exposure. The published result identifies syconium size as the principal determinant of seed and wasp production; asynchrony is used mainly to interpret year-round pollinator persistence and a more seed-biased seed:female-pollinator allocation ratio relative to synchronous fig systems.

Thus it does not provide:

1. a focal-season adult-pollinator availability curve;
2. plant timing groups prospectively ordered by overlap with that curve;
3. matched post-cost seed output for those timing groups.

Cross-species comparison of asynchronous versus synchronous figs is not a substitute for a within-programme timing contrast.

## Consequence

The Ficus literature remains useful for the conceptual distinction between:

- host internal flowering synchrony;
- partner population persistence;
- seed-versus-pollinator offspring allocation.

But none of the audited Ficus sources closes the current mixed IWE estimand.

`MIX002_FICUS_AUREA` is therefore moved from `blocked_final_surface` to `rejected_other` / `DROP`, and the generic fig search stratum remains closed unless a new study explicitly links partner availability, host timing, and post-cost seed production at a common biological unit.
