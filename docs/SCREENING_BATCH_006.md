# Screening batch 006 — mixed nursery-pollination systems

Date: 2026-09-17
Scope: IWE054–IWE058

## Purpose

This batch tested whether the measurement split previously seen in Silene–Hadena generalizes across classic nursery-pollination systems.

The strict target remained unchanged:

```text
plant-partner temporal matching
-> final plant reproductive fitness
-> effect + sampling uncertainty
```

## Trollius–Chiastocheta

### IWE054 — Hemborg & Després 1999

Annual seed output, fly oviposition and larval seed predation are quantified across Trollius plants, altitudes and flower-number states. This is a strong final-fitness cost/benefit study. The focal predictors, however, are flower size/number, altitude and fly abundance, not temporal overlap between plant flowering and fly activity.

### IWE055 — Després & Jaeger 1999

Six Chiastocheta species partition oviposition across flower developmental ages from first-day flowers through fruit stages. The resulting plant outcome ranges from mutualistic to purely parasitic depending on fly species and oviposition strategy. This demonstrates timing within a flower's lifespan, but not seasonal plant-partner phenological matching suitable for IWE H1.

### IWE056 — Ibáñez et al. 2009

Experimental opening of globeflowers changes visitation by Chiastocheta and co-pollinators and changes net seed set/pollen export. The experiment is valuable because plant fitness is measured, but floral architecture rather than timing is manipulated.

## Glochidion–Epicephala

### IWE057 — Goto et al. 2010

`Glochidion acuminatum` responds to Epicephala egg load and ovule damage by selective flower abortion. The mechanism increases expected seed production by about 16% relative to random abortion, directly linking nursery-pollinator exploitation to plant seed fitness. Yet temporal matching is not the predictor. This study therefore supplies the **fitness side** of the mixed-interaction chain, not the synchrony side.

This complements IWE017, where `Breynia oblongifolia × Epicephala` phenology is measured in detail but final synchrony-to-plant-fitness effects are not.

## Dianthus–Hadena

### IWE058 — Collin et al. 2002

Pollination, oviposition, fruit/seed predation and sex-morph reproductive consequences are quantified in `Dianthus sylvestris`. The primary axes are flower size, sex morph and natural-enemy exposure. Phenology is not the quantitative interaction axis. This paper is biologically linked to IWE041 but cannot be pooled as a timing effect.

## Review-level support for the measurement-gap diagnosis

Kephart et al. (2006), DOI `10.1111/j.1469-8137.2005.01619.x`, reviewed more than 30 Caryophyllaceae taxa and >50 case studies of Hadena/Perizoma nursery pollination. The review explicitly states that accurate classification of these interactions requires more complete information on pollinator frequency/effectiveness, seed-predation pressure, effects on seed set and selection. This is historical evidence that the full benefit–cost measurement chain was already recognized as incomplete; it is not itself an empirical IWE study.

## Batch result

The mixed-system measurement split is not confined to Hadena:

```text
Trollius: final seed output + oviposition costs, but no seasonal synchrony effect
Epicephala: host/pollinator tracking in one literature branch; seed-fitness regulation in another
Hadena/Dianthus/Silene: seasonal timing and final fitness often measured, but independent moth activity is usually absent
```

No IWE054–058 study closes the strict H1 chain.

After source-level adjudication, IWE014 and IWE019 are also removed from `unresolved strict` status: IWE014 lacks an independent partner-activity timing exposure, whereas IWE019 directly measures wasp/fig timing but does not estimate a comparable synchrony-to-final-seed effect with uncertainty.

Thus the current mixed strict-H1 lane has **zero admitted or unresolved-strict effects**, while retaining a substantial mechanism/timing literature.
