# Hemborg & Després 1999 — within-plant timing costs are buffered at annual seed output

Date: 2026-10-05  
Study: Hemborg & Després 1999, *Trollius europaeus × Chiastocheta* spp.  
DOI: `10.1007/s004420050875`  
Propagation role: independent mixed-system example in which a timing-associated predation signal is buffered before whole-plant annual fitness.

## Stage/timing structure

Multi-flowered *Trollius europaeus* plants produce a large first flower followed by smaller secondary flowers.

The source expected later secondary flowers to keep attracting flies around the same plant and thereby increase oviposition pressure on the first flower.

Consistent with that mechanism:

- early first flowers on multi-flowered plants attracted more ovipositing flies than single flowers;
- those early flowers experienced higher relative seed predation.

Thus within-plant reproductive timing altered realized mixed-interaction cost.

## Final annual plant output does not preserve the simple cost signal

The higher predation on early multiple flowers did **not** generally make multi-flowered plants worse off.

The original study reports that multi-flowered plants had greater annual seed output than single-flowered plants at every sampled altitude in Sweden and the French Alps.

A later quantitative restatement of the same field result reports approximately **80–140% higher seed output** in multi-flowered plants.

The plant-level signal is therefore:

`early-flower oviposition/predation cost -> buffered by additional floral/reproductive output -> higher whole-plant annual seed production`.

## Why this matters for temporal signal propagation

This is not a null at the interaction stage. Timing is biologically meaningful: early flowers receive more oviposition and greater relative seed predation.

The signal changes only when the response is integrated to the plant's annual reproductive scale.

Therefore the same programme illustrates a core IWE rule:

> a temporal interaction signal can be real at one reproductive unit and yet be buffered when fitness is summed across multiple reproductive units.

This is especially relevant to mixed systems, because pollination benefit, seed-predation cost, flower number and allocation can compensate each other before annual fitness is realized.

## Relation to other Trollius evidence

Other Trollius–Chiastocheta work shows strong stage specialization among fly species:

- early species oviposit on young flowers and contribute strongly to pollination;
- late species oviposit later and can contribute less marginal pollination per oviposition.

Després 2004 additionally reports similar post-predation intact-seed summaries for early- versus late-species contexts (140.4 vs 146.6 seeds), despite major differences in oviposition stage and larval ecology.

Together these results caution against treating fly oviposition stage as an invariant whole-plant fitness coefficient.
