# IWE075 extraction receipt — Scheelea zonensis × Caryobruchus giganteus

Source: Wright SJ. 1990. *Cumulative satiation of a seed predator over the fruiting season of its host*. Oikos 58:272–276. DOI `10.2307/3545217`.

Status: **unresolved strict-H1 antagonist candidate; crop-level quantitative recovery required**.

## Biological timing chain

The host palm `Scheelea zonensis` (now often treated within `Attalea`) fruits over a season on Barro Colorado Island, Panama. The bruchid `Caryobruchus giganteus` oviposits on the endocarp; larval development occurs inside the seed and kills it.

The source directly reports a seasonal antagonist window:

- oviposition is relatively high early and near the host fruiting peak;
- oviposition declines through the season;
- it reaches zero while about 20% of palms are still ripening fruit.

The insect is effectively univoltine in this system because larval development exceeds the host fruiting season, so adult numbers are greatest near the beginning of the host season.

Thus late seed crops occupy a temporal refuge from the realized antagonist oviposition window.

## Why this could satisfy strict H1

Unlike a study that records only flowering/fruiting date and later damage, the source explicitly measures the animal oviposition process through the host reproductive season and links oviposition to seed mortality.

If crop- or date-level source data include both:

- timing/oviposition exposure; and
- numbers or proportions of surviving seeds,

then a direct antagonist timing-to-final-seed effect can be reconstructed.

## Why no meta row is entered yet

The publicly accessible abstract and citation records do not expose:

- crop-level oviposition counts/proportions;
- crop-level killed vs surviving seeds;
- sample sizes and sampling variance by date/crop.

The statement that late crops escape and the fact that oviposition reaches zero before host fruiting ends are not enough to construct a sampling variance.

No effect is reverse-engineered from narrative percentages or significance claims.

If only a predation-rate response is recoverable without final surviving-seed information, move the study to context rather than treating attack as plant fitness.
