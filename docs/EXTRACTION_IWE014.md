# IWE014 extraction receipt — Silene vulgaris × Hadena

Source: Pettersson MW. 1991. *Flower herbivory and seed predation in Silene vulgaris (Caryophyllaceae): effects of pollination and phenology*. Holarctic Ecology / Ecography 14:45–50. DOI `10.1111/j.1600-0587.1991.tb00632.x`.

Status: **context / direct seasonal timing; not strict H1**.

## What the study directly measures

The study compares two flowering seasons with contrasting pollinator abundance. Noctuid moths of `Hadena` are both flower visitors/pollinators and seed predators. The source reports:

- percentage of flowers pollinated;
- flowers/fruits lost to Hadena larvae;
- seed capsules destroyed;
- final seed set;
- early versus late flowering position within the season.

The biological result is unusually relevant to IWE: pollination benefit and seed-predation cost covary because the same moth guild contributes to both. In the low-pollinator year, lower pollination was counterbalanced by lower Hadena flower/capsule destruction, producing similar fruit numbers and higher seed set. Across the flowering season, pollination and capsule destruction were positively associated, and early-flowering individuals had similar pollination success but more seed predation than later individuals.

## Why it is not strict H1

The source does **not** provide an independently quantified `Hadena` activity/emergence distribution that can be paired with plant flowering timing to construct a registered overlap or mismatch exposure.

The available exposure is therefore:

```text
plant seasonal position / year-specific moth regime
```

rather than:

```text
plant-Hadena temporal matching
```

under IWE's strict timing contract.

The accessible article/abstract/JSTOR metadata also do not expose a recoverable synchrony coefficient with sampling variance. A qualitative positive correlation between pollination and capsule destruction is not converted into a numerical effect.

## Adjudication

IWE014 moves from `unresolved` to `context_only` for strict H1.

It remains one of the strongest **mixed seasonal-timing** anchors because it connects pollination, seed predation and final seed set in one biological system.

## Claim ceiling

The study supports:

> seasonal position and moth regime jointly change pollination benefit, seed-predation cost and final seed production in a mixed pollinating-seed-predator system.

It does not identify:

- a common-scale plant-partner synchrony slope;
- an absolute mismatch effect;
- an independent quantitative estimate suitable for `direct_effects.csv`.
