# Northern Yucca: reported reversal in which egg load predicts fruit retention

Date: 2026-10-08
System: *Yucca glauca × Tegeticula yuccasella*
Status: **ecological boundary / hypothesis-generating, secondary-source documented**;
not a new strict-H1 effect, final-fitness synchrony comparison, or independently
recovered thesis-level observation.

## Why this matters

The usual "partner sanctions" expectation in the classic yucca–moth mutualism
is that flowers/fruit receiving excessive moth eggs or ovipositor damage are
preferentially aborted, protecting plant seeds from excessive larval costs.

The Canadian 2013 COSEWIC assessment, explicitly citing Hurlburt (2004),
instead reports a northern-periphery **reverse selective abscission**:
Soapweed plants at Alberta's Onefour population tend to abort flowers with
*fewer* moth ovipositions, retaining fruit linked to more moth eggs. Its
companion Soapweed assessment says flowers with low pollen or few ovipositions
were more likely to abort. The report contrasts this with sanction-like
abortion of egg-rich fruits in many other yuccas.

## Two biological mechanisms must not be conflated

1. **Service-linked selection of reproductive units:** the pollinating
   female moth transfers pollen and oviposits in the same visit. Low egg
   receipt may mark insufficient pollen delivery rather than plant
   preference for larval consumers. A positive egg–retention correlation is
   not proof that plants gain fitness *from more eggs*.
2. **Subsequent host and moth filtering:** Jadeja et al. (2017,
   DOI 10.1002/ece3.3426) experimentally show moths avoid ovipositing in
   late distal *Y. glauca* flowers when basal fruits already impose a
   high risk of abortion. Host resource allocation therefore changes
   which eggs survive, and moth behaviour responds in advance. That
   experiment is a **different programme** and does not establish the
   northern egg-number/retention relationship at the same units.

Together these sources raise a more biological IWE question:

> Does the effect of egg-bearing interactions on plant reproductive
> retention change sign as effective *pollination service* changes from
> limiting to abundant relative to later larval seed-consumption costs?

The source does **not** demonstrate a within-species geographic flip:
the usual sanction contrast includes different *Yucca* species,
populations and experimental contexts. Nor is there evidence that
northern plants deliberately favour larval survival.

## Falsifiable discriminator for a future source

At matched flowering-date/fruit-position units, jointly observe or
manipulate (a) maternal pollen delivery or pollen supplementation,
(b) egg load/oviposition injury, (c) distal/basal resource sinks,
(d) floral abortion, and (e) **intact mature seeds per initially
available plant flower**. An egg-retention association that reverses
with pollen availability, but vanishes on experimentally holding pollen
delivery constant, would support **egg load as a pollination cue** over
an intrinsic preference for high consumer load. A persistent egg-load
effect after pollen and resource controls would require a different
mechanistic account, including tissue damage and consumer survival.

This proposal is testable but is **not** an inference from existing
IWE source tables. The Hurlburt programme still lacks the thesis
join key mapping flowering/independently censused adults to mature-fruit
and viable-seed outcomes. Annual moth density, egg count, and
year-aggregated viable seeds are not substitutes for synchrony.

## Evidence status

- Northern reverse-abscission pattern: **reported by official secondary
  status assessments citing Hurlburt (2004); primary raw contrast not
  re-extracted**.
- Behavioural avoidance of future-aborted host positions: **direct
  experimental source, Jadeja et al. (2017)**.
- Pollination-limitation-by-egg-load sign switch to final net fitness:
  **untested hypothesis**.
- Independent matched timing-to-final seed production and quantitative
  mixed-H1 SMD cluster: **absent**.
