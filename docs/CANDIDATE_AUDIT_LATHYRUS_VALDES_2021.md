# Candidate audit — Lathyrus vernus flowering time × antagonistic interactions

Date: 2026-09-28  
Study ID: `IWE035`  
Source: Valdés & Ehrlén (2021), *Ecology* 102:e03466  
DOI: `10.1002/ecy.3466`  
Decision: **context-only; plant fitness is measured but the independent partner-timing reference is missing**

## Why this source entered the screen

The study uses 21 years of data on the perennial herb *Lathyrus vernus* and jointly models:

- first flowering date;
- vertebrate grazing;
- predispersal seed predation;
- probability of producing seed;
- number of intact seeds.

This makes it stronger than many antagonist phenology papers on the plant-fitness side.

The published structural-equation analysis explicitly models intact seed production and shows that both grazing and seed predation can reduce plant fitness.

## Strict synchrony blocker

The IWE strict synchrony estimand requires animal activity/availability to order plant timing independently of the realized plant–animal interaction outcome.

In this study, the relevant antagonist variables are:

- grazing intensity experienced by individual plants; and
- seed-predation intensity experienced by individual plants.

Those are realized interaction/damage quantities. The study discusses climate-driven changes in animal seasonal activity and relative timing conceptually, but it does not provide a contemporaneous animal activity curve that can prospectively classify each flowering window as more or less synchronized.

Therefore IWE will not use grazing or seed-predation intensity as the missing partner-availability curve.

## Gate status

| gate | status |
|---|---|
| direct plant flowering timing | yes |
| final plant reproductive fitness | yes |
| antagonistic interaction intensity | yes |
| independently measured partner activity window | **no** |
| strict synchrony effect | **no** |
| screening decision | **context_only** |

## Interpretation

IWE035 is useful direct seasonal-timing/fitness context: it demonstrates that antagonistic interactions can mediate flowering-time selection and affect intact-seed fitness over long time series.

It does not answer the strict IWE question of how **greater plant–animal phenological synchrony itself** changes plant reproductive performance, because the partner timing axis is not independently measured.
