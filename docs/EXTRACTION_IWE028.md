# IWE028 extraction receipt — Albrectsen 2000

Source: Albrectsen Riber B. 2000. *Flowering phenology and seed predation by a tephritid fly: Escape of seeds in time and space*. Écoscience 7:433–438. DOI `10.1080/11956860.2000.11682614`.

Status: **high-information antagonist timing context; strict H1 not closed**.

## Biological design

The monocarpic host `Tripolium vulgare` has an extended flowering season. The specialist tephritid `Paroxyna plantaginis` attacks developing reproductive structures.

The source transplanted plants to eight islands and removed plants successively through the season, comparing the temporal attack pattern with eleven natural populations. Early terminal flower heads flower first; later lateral heads provide an ordered within-plant phenology.

The paper reports that early terminal heads:

- had higher potential seed set;
- had lower attack probability in the normal/high-attack year;
- benefited from a phenological lag between first flowering and the emergence/density build-up of ovipositing flies.

Thus the source gives strong direct evidence for temporal escape from an antagonist.

## Why strict Tier A is not admitted

IWE Tier A requires a directly observed plant reproductive/fitness outcome matched to the timing exposure with a recoverable effect and sampling variance.

The accessible source description and methods recover:

- flower-head phenological rank;
- potential seed set / ovule number;
- attack by `P. plantaginis`;
- temporal/spatial attack risk.

The integrated `realized seed set` treatment used elsewhere in the associated thesis combines potential seed set and attack/survival components in a model rather than supplying an observed final-fitness response on the same units. That model output is biologically useful but does not satisfy the current IWE primary contract.

No effect is therefore added to `direct_effects.csv`.

## Evidence role

Retain IWE028 as:

- direct antagonist phenology/attack evidence;
- support for temporal enemy escape;
- a candidate mechanism/timing-sensitivity record;
- **not** a strict synchrony-to-observed-fitness effect.

## Claim boundary

The study supports the interpretation that early flowering can escape a lagging seed predator. It does not supply the strict IWE estimand required for a common-scale H1 meta-analysis.

This negative admission decision is important evidence about the literature itself: even classic phenological-enemy studies often stop at attack or modelled fitness rather than directly measured final reproductive success.
