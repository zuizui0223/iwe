# Mixed candidate audit — Breynia oblongifolia × Epicephala

Date: 2026-09-25  
Candidate: `MIX002_BREYNIA_EPICEPHALA`  
Programme: Finch et al. 2018–2021, Richmond NSW  
Decision: **reject from strict mixed #2; same-programme timing and final-fitness surfaces are not linked**

## Why the programme looked promising

The Finch programme contains unusually complete ingredients across three linked papers:

- direct nocturnal observations of pollinating and ovipositing *Epicephala* at Richmond;
- date-resolved host flowering/fruiting and adult moth phenology over >1 year;
- fruit-level counts of developed seeds, seeds consumed by pollinators/parasites, and net mature seed production;
- repeated Richmond fruit crops, including Autumn 2018.

At first glance this looks like a rare route to the full IWE chain.

## The decisive unit-level break

The 2018 pollination-observation protocol states that once a moth had been observed pollinating and ovipositing into a female flower, **both moth and flower were removed and taken to the laboratory**. Those observed flowers were dissected for pollen and eggs.

They therefore cannot mature into the fruits used in the separate herbivory/final-seed dataset.

The 2019 fruit study instead haphazardly sampled mature fruits from plants by site/crop. It records fruit-level developed and consumed seeds, but not the original pollination/flowering date of each fruit.

Thus there is no source-backed mapping:

`observed/date-resolved Epicephala activity -> focal reproductive unit -> mature post-cost seed output`.

## Why crop-level matching does not rescue the effect

Richmond Autumn 2018 fruits occur after the September 2017–April 2018 observation period, and the programme clearly establishes Epicephala activity during that broad period. But this supplies only one contemporaneously observed fruit crop.

Richmond Autumn 2017 final fruits are also available in the herbivory dataset, but the direct pollination-observation series had not begun during the corresponding pollination period. Comparing Autumn 2017 versus Autumn 2018 would therefore violate the same contemporaneous-window rule used elsewhere in IWE.

The later 2021 phenology study adds a 75-week activity curve but does not recover the missing pollination-week provenance for the mature fruits already sampled in the 2019 study.

## Gate decision

| gate | status |
|---|---|
| independent programme | yes |
| same-season partner activity measured | yes |
| final post-consumption plant reproduction measured | yes |
| timing exposure linked to final reproductive unit | **no** |
| SMD-ready strict contrast | **no** |
| status | **rejected_timing** |

This is a resolved design incompatibility, not a temporary data-download problem.

## Consequence for the Epicephala search

Other *Epicephala–Phyllanthaceae* studies often show very close generational tracking of host flowering and can quantify the fraction of seeds consumed versus left intact. That demonstrates synchrony and net cost biology, but it does not itself provide **variation in synchrony** linked to reproductive outcome.

The mixed #2 search should therefore not keep treating generic Epicephala phenology papers as near-ready candidates. A qualifying study needs explicit within-programme variation in plant–moth overlap plus final post-cost reproduction at the same unit.
