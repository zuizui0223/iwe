# IWE072 extraction receipt — Carduus pycnocephalus × Rhinocyllus conicus

Source: Goeden RD, Ricker DW. 1985. *Seasonal Asynchrony of Italian Thistle, Carduus pycnocephalus, and the Weevil, Rhinocyllus conicus (Coleoptera: Curculionidae), Introduced for Biological Control in Southern California*. Environmental Entomology 14:433–436. DOI `10.1093/ee/14.4.433`.

Status: **unresolved strict-H1 candidate; full-paper quantitative recovery required**.

## Source-level biological chain

The study followed 30 Italian thistle plants through a long flowering season from mid-April to late August 1980.

The accessible abstract reports:

- 7,735 flower heads in total (mean 258 per plant);
- approximately 25,000 potential seeds;
- univoltine `R. conicus` oviposition ended in late June;
- weevil larvae caused an estimated 55% seed loss;
- 11,410 seeds nevertheless survived;
- most surviving seeds were produced **after weevil oviposition had ended**.

This is direct evidence of a temporal refuge: the host reproductive window extended beyond the antagonist oviposition window, and substantial final seed output accumulated in that low-overlap period.

## Why totals are insufficient for a strict quantitative effect

The abstract provides season totals but not the before-versus-after-oviposition sample structure, variance, or individual/head-level reproductive distribution.

Therefore IWE does not create a log response ratio from:

`11,410 surviving seeds / 25,000 potential seeds`

because this is not a contrast between matched and mismatched timing states.

Nor is the estimated 55% seed loss itself a synchrony effect.

## Quantitative closure route

Recover the original tables/figures and determine whether they provide:

- numbers of heads/seeds before versus after the end of oviposition;
- plant-level or head-level seed production by date;
- variance or raw sample sizes for those temporal groups.

If recoverable, define the timing contrast **prospectively from the weevil oviposition boundary** (overlap before late June versus escape after late June), then estimate final viable-seed output on that same temporal partition.

## Claim ceiling

IWE072 currently supports:

> Much of the host's realized seed production occurred after the seasonal antagonist oviposition window closed.

It does not yet provide a pooled strict-H1 effect size or variance.
