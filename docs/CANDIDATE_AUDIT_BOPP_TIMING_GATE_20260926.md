# Bopp 2003/2004 timing-gate recheck

Date: 2026-09-26  
Candidate: `MIX002_BOPP_2004`  
System: *Silene latifolia* ssp. *alba* / *S. dioica* × *Hadena bicruris*  
Decision: demote from P1 `blocked_final_surface` to P2 `blocked_timing_linkage`

## Why the previous classification was too permissive

The 2004 Oikos paper is unusually attractive for IWE because it presents plant flowering phenology and a same-season curve labelled as moth activity/oviposition phenology.

A methods recheck shows that the field series does **not** come from an independent census or trap-based measure of adult moth availability. Female flowers were repeatedly opened and newly deposited *H. bicruris* eggs since the preceding observation were counted. The paper then used the daily new-flower and new-egg series to compare host flowering with the period of moth activity.

That is direct oviposition phenology, but the egg series is still a realized plant–moth interaction outcome. The number of eggs received by flowers depends on at least:

- whether suitable host flowers are available;
- host-species and flower choice by female moths;
- successful oviposition after encounter;
- the underlying number/activity of adult moths.

It therefore cannot, by itself, provide the independent partner-availability curve required to order plant timing prospectively under the frozen strict-window contract.

This is the same fail-closed logic already applied to *Dianthus sylvestris × Hadena compta*, *Rheum nobile × Bradysia*, and antagonist candidates where infestation or attack cannot be recycled as the predator-activity window.

## What the 2004 article does support

The source robustly supports several biological statements:

- *S. latifolia* and *S. dioica* flowering were followed through the season;
- newly deposited *H. bicruris* eggs were recorded repeatedly;
- oviposition on *S. latifolia* was concentrated around its flowering peaks;
- *S. dioica* functioned mainly as an alternative oviposition host when *S. latifolia* flower supply was low;
- larval performance differed between host species.

Those results are useful nursery-pollination and host-choice evidence. They are not enough for the strict IWE exposure.

The published plant-side response is also not a post-cost final reproductive endpoint: the paper emphasizes host choice and larval/pupal performance rather than final plant seed or fruit production after seed predation.

## Status of the 2003 monograph

Bopp (2003), *Parasitismus oder Symbiose? Beziehungen zwischen einem parasitischen Bestäuber (Hadena bicruris) und seinen Wirtspflanzen*, Zoologica 152, is catalogue-confirmed as a 140-page monograph with 36 tables.

The monograph remains worth one targeted long-form audit because it could, in principle, contain measurements omitted from the shorter Oikos paper.

However, it is no longer enough to recover only a final seed/fruit table. The programme can return to P1 only if the long-form source adds **both**:

1. an independent focal-season adult-moth activity/availability series not inferred from eggs received by host flowers; and
2. a linked final post-seed-predation plant reproductive endpoint with variance at a defensible independent unit.

If either component is absent, Bopp remains outside strict H1.
