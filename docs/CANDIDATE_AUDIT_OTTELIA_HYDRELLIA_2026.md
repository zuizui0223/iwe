Journal: New Phytologist  
DOI: `10.1111/nph.71476`  
Data: public Figshare share linked by the article  
Candidate: `MIX002_OTTELIA_HYDRELLIA_2026`  
Decision: **context-only / rejected_other for the frozen mixed pollinating-seed-predator class**

## Why this paper belongs in the IWE boundary audit

The paper reports a newly described aquatic nursery-pollination interaction.

In *Ottelia jingxiensis*:

- *Hydrellia* shore flies are primary pollinators;
- rewardless female flowers attract flies via imperfect Bakerian mimicry;
- flies oviposit on persistent female calyces;
- larvae later leave the reproductive structure and migrate to submerged leaves;
- foliar herbivory is substantial;
- a parasitoid wasp, *Chaenusa naiadum*, parasitises many pupae and also provides supplementary pollination.

Thus adult benefit and larval cost are both demonstrated within a nursery-pollination system.

## Why it is not a pollinating seed predator in the frozen IWE sense

The key mechanistic result is **spatial decoupling**.

The pollinator's larvae do not remain in fruits or seeds. They migrate to vegetative leaves and feed there.

The study reports that c. 62% of leaves have more than 30% area consumed, but this foliar herbivory is essentially unrelated to seed set (`R² < 0.001`).

Therefore the larval cost is not a direct post-pollination consumption of seeds, ovules or fruit reproductive tissue on the same final female reproductive surface.

This differs from systems such as Hadena, Tegeticula, Epicephala, Greya or the 2026 *Ludwigia–Nanophyes* system, where larval development can directly consume reproductive output.

## Timing gate

The study contains rich floral and insect observations, but its primary contrasts concern:

- female-flower mimicry and pollination;
- oviposition on calyces;
- migration of larvae to leaves;
- foliar herbivory tolerance;
- parasitoid effects.

It does not provide the IWE primary exposure: variation in plant flowering timing prospectively ordered by a contemporaneous adult-fly activity window and linked to final seed production.

Thus it also would not pass the strict timing gate even if the biological cost surface were broadened.

## Why this should not be forced into IWE

The paper is ecologically important precisely because it shows a different solution to nursery-pollination conflict: pollination and herbivory are spatially separated, and the plant tolerates leaf loss.

Reclassifying this as a pollinating seed-predator system would erase that biological novelty and weaken the IWE estimand.

The correct IWE use is as boundary/context evidence that nursery pollination is broader than the focal mixed class.

## Search consequence

This paper was published online on 31 July 2026, so adding it verifies that the refreshed targeted search reaches very recent nursery-pollination discoveries.

Together with IWE033 (*Ludwigia–Nanophyes*), it also shows two distinct reasons why "nursery pollination" alone is insufficient for the frozen IWE mixed class:

1. a genuine reproductive-tissue cost can be present but no plant–partner timing contrast exists (IWE033);
2. the nursery interaction can be genuine but the larval cost occurs on vegetative tissue rather than the final reproductive surface (IWE034).
