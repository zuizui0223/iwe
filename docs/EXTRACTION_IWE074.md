# IWE074 extraction receipt — salt-marsh perennial community × seasonal insect consumers

Source: Bertness MD, Wise C, Ellison AM. 1987. *Consumer pressure and seed set in a salt marsh perennial plant community*. Oecologia 71:190–200. DOI `10.1007/BF00377284`.

Status: **unresolved strict-H1 antagonist candidate; source table/variance recovery required**.

## Why the design is close to strict H1

The study directly links three components in the same marsh community:

1. seasonal insect-consumer abundance/pressure;
2. species-specific flowering phenology;
3. final seed set under natural consumer pressure and consumer exclusion.

The animal community was dominated by the generalist grasshopper `Conocephalus spartinae`, with additional planthoppers and larval seed parasites.

The reported seasonal interaction window is clear:

- consumer abundance/pressure increased through early summer;
- it peaked in mid-July;
- it declined through August.

Plant flowering positions span that window:

- `Juncus gerardi` flowers early and largely escapes;
- `Spartina patens` and `Distichlis spicata` flower near peak consumer activity and suffer severe losses;
- `Spartina alterniflora` flowers later as consumer pressure declines.

Direct ovule loss ranged from 51–80%, with seed-set reductions from about 50% to more than twenty-fold.

## Why no quantitative H1 row is entered

The currently accessible public text exposes the qualitative/aggregate pattern but not the full original table required to align, at compatible grain:

- consumer activity by date;
- reproductive availability/flowering by species/date;
- final seed set or seeds/flower;
- treatment/sample sizes;
- SD/SE or raw replicate values.

The full-text ResearchGate entry is indexed, but retrieval in the current environment is rate-limited. The PubMed abstract confirms the design and aggregate effects but does not provide sampling variance.

A species ranking such as early/mid/late cannot be converted into a quantitative overlap effect after seeing the seed-set outcome.

## Strict closure route

If the original tables/raw values become available, freeze the reconstruction before calculating the result:

1. digitize/use the published consumer-pressure curve only if its dates and scale are explicit;
2. construct each plant species' reproductive-availability curve from source flowering data;
3. calculate a declared overlap index between plant reproductive availability and the consumer-pressure curve;
4. use final seed set from the same source/treatment;
5. preserve species as dependent rows from one community programme;
6. derive sampling variance only from reported/raw uncertainty.

A simpler experimental contrast is possible only if the source independently defines high-consumer-overlap and low-consumer-overlap states with seed-set means and uncertainty.

## Important limitation

This is a **consumer guild**, not a one-to-one species pair. IWE may admit it only if the animal activity curve represents the biologically relevant reproductive consumers measured in the source and the interaction class is kept as an antagonist guild. It must not be relabelled as `Conocephalus` alone unless the source data isolate that species.

## Claim ceiling

Current evidence supports:

> plant species flowering at different positions relative to a strongly seasonal marsh-consumer window experienced markedly different reproductive losses.

It does not yet supply a recoverable strict-H1 effect size and sampling variance.
