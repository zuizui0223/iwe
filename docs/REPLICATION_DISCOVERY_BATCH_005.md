## Yucca filifera × Tegeticula yuccasella — P1

Rentería & Cantú (2003), *Acta Zoológica Mexicana* 89:85–92  
DOI: `10.21829/azm.2003.89891776`

This study was missed by the earlier family-level Yucca screen because its title is in Spanish and its quantitative design is clearer in the full text than in generic nursery-pollination searches.

### Timing exposure

The focal 1998 field season contains a real same-season partner window:

- reproductive phenology was followed on 20 Yucca plants; 15 flowered and produced 47 inflorescences;
- adult *T. yuccasella* density was sampled with 11 nocturnal UV-trap sessions from 5 March to 18 May;
- moth density was highest in March, coincident with the high mature-flower period;
- moths were collected in March and April but **none were collected in May despite flowers still being present**.

This passes the partner-window gate. The high-versus-low ordering is not inferred from seed damage.

### Final reproductive surface

The study also samples the post-interaction mature seed surface:

- 500 mature fruits were scored;
- mean viable mature seeds per fruit = 64.2 ± 28.6;
- mean mature damaged seeds per fruit = 3.8 ± 7.0;
- only 83/500 mature fruits showed holes attributed to moth descendants/possible parasitoids.

Thus the study includes final viable seed production after larval seed consumption.

### Why no strict SMD is extracted

The mature-fruit seed dataset is pooled across the season. The publication does not retain, for each mature fruit or reproductive inflorescence, the flowering date/month that would assign it to the measured moth-activity window.

Therefore IWE cannot legitimately form:

`March high moth overlap final seeds` versus `May zero-moth overlap final seeds`.

Doing so would require inventing the provenance of mature fruits.

Decision: `blocked_timing_linkage`, P1.

Unlock only with a source-backed fruit/inflorescence flowering-date label, a month/cohort-specific final-seed table, or raw data that link the mature fruit to its flowering window.

## Syagrus coronata × Anchylorhynchus trapezicollis — reject strict completion route

de Medeiros et al. (2019), *Biological Journal of the Linnean Society*  
DOI: `10.1093/biolinnean/blz008`

This is clearly a true brood-pollinator biology: *A. trapezicollis* is an important pollinator and its larvae develop in fruits as pre-dispersal seed predators.

However, the focal fruit counts were made only a few days after fertilization. The paper explicitly notes that those counts **do not include subsequent fruit abortion caused by Anchylorhynchus larvae**.

A separate *S. coronata* reproductive-biology study monitors blooming phenology for a year and identifies floral visitors. That does not solve the problem: IWE does not splice a partner-window component from one publication and a different non-post-cost outcome surface into a synthetic strict effect.

Decision: `rejected_other` for the replication target.

## Butia / Syagrus petraea literature

The Cerrado-palm literature confirms that *Anchylorhynchus* adults can pollinate while larvae consume pistillate reproductive tissue; for example *A. bicolor* on *Butia paraguayensis* may attack 30–50% of pistillate flowers.

These papers are useful mixed-system natural history but do not provide a within-plant more-versus-less phenological synchrony contrast tied to final post-cost reproduction. They are not added as near-ready ledger entries.

The next useful action is not another broad taxonomic search. It is to recover the missing linkage/surface inside one of these three programmes.
