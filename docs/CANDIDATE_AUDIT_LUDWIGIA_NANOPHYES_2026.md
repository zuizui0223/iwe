# Candidate audit — Ludwigia adscendens × Nanophyes proles

Date: 2026-09-29  
Source: Wu & Chen (2026), *A novel nursery pollination mutualism between a water primrose and its fruit-parasitizing weevil*  
Journal: Ecology 107:e70440  
DOI: `10.1002/ecy.70440`  
Data: Figshare DOI `10.6084/m9.figshare.30831134.v1`  
Candidate: `MIX002_LUDWIGIA_NANOPHYES_2026`  
Decision: **context-only / rejected_timing for strict IWE**

## Why this new paper matters

This paper was published in June 2026 and documents a genuinely mixed nursery-pollination system outside the canonical fig/yucca/Hadena/Epicephala families.

In natural *Ludwigia adscendens* populations in Guangxi:

- adult *Nanophyes proles* visit flowers and act as effective pollinators;
- adults feed and mate on flowers;
- females oviposit into ovaries about 2–3 days before anthesis;
- larvae develop inside fruits and consume seeds;
- final seed costs rise strongly with larval number;
- fruit submergence kills a subset of developing weevils and preserves additional seeds.

The study therefore has a real benefit–cost nursery-pollination surface and public data.

## Final reproductive cost is not the blocker

The public study summary reports two years of parasitism and direct seed loss.

Approximately one larva consumes about one third of seeds, while fruits containing three larvae can lose more than 95% of their seeds. Fruits in which developing weevils drown after submergence retain more seeds.

Thus the plant-side cost is measured on a final reproductive surface rather than merely as visitation or egg receipt.

## Why it fails the IWE timing estimand

The study does not define a plant phenology contrast such as early versus late flowering, or more versus less overlap, that is prospectively ordered by an independently measured seasonal adult-weevil activity window.

Relevant timing facts include:

- the population flowers from May to September;
- individual flowers last one day;
- females oviposit before anthesis;
- fruits submerge roughly 3–4 weeks after flowering.

These are life-history timing observations, not a between-plant synchrony exposure.

The primary mechanistic contrasts concern:

- pollinator contribution;
- parasitism/larval number;
- seed consumption;
- fruit submergence and weevil survival.

IWE must not relabel larval number, parasitism, pre-anthesis oviposition, flower lifespan, or fruit-submergence timing as a high-versus-low plant–partner synchrony contrast.

## Data availability

The article explicitly releases data through Figshare at DOI `10.6084/m9.figshare.30831134.v1`.

This makes the system useful for future questions about nursery-pollination cost–benefit balance, sanctions and aquatic mutualism stability. Data availability does not repair the missing IWE timing estimand.

## Search consequence

This 2026 paper demonstrates that the updated search is reaching newly described nursery-pollination systems, not only canonical legacy systems.

It also reproduces the dominant IWE failure mode: rich pollination, parasitism and final-fitness data can coexist without the **plant timing × independently measured partner-availability** contrast required for strict synchrony inference.
