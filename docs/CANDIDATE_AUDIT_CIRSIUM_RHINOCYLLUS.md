# Candidate audit — Cirsium canescens × Rhinocyllus conicus

Date: 2026-09-25  
Timing source: Russell & Louda (2004), *Oecologia* 139:525–534, DOI `10.1007/s00442-004-1543-1`  
Final-fitness programme source: Rose, Louda & Rees (2005), *Ecology* 86:453–465, DOI `10.1890/03-0697`  
Candidate: `ANT002_CIRSIUM_RHINOCYLLUS`  
Decision: **P2 `blocked_timing_linkage`; do not splice the published models**

## Why the timing gate is unusually strong

Russell & Louda directly measured both partners in the same focal seasons.

Across Platte thistle populations in Nebraska during 1997–2000 they quantified:

- within-season adult *Rhinocyllus conicus* activity;
- within-season *Cirsium canescens* flower-head development;
- a source-defined synchrony/asynchrony metric based on the difference between the proportion of adult weevils observed and the proportion of flower heads initiated by late May.

The source explicitly shows that variation in synchrony predicts egg load. This is genuine partner-window evidence, not attack used as a surrogate for partner phenology.

## Final reproduction exists in the same long-term programme

The Platte-thistle programme also contains strong plant-fitness information.

Rose, Louda & Rees analyze 11 years of individual demography and model seed set as a function of plant size, native insect damage and *Rhinocyllus* egg load. Earlier programme results report severe viable-seed loss in heads with the introduced weevil.

Thus this is not a case where final plant fitness was never measured.

## Why it still cannot enter the frozen SMD family

The public sources expose two linked but statistically distinct surfaces:

1. `phenological synchrony -> Rhinocyllus egg load`;
2. `Rhinocyllus egg load / damage -> seed set`.

IWE's primary effect must be a source-backed effect of **greater synchrony on final plant reproduction**. Multiplying, composing or otherwise propagating these two published models would create a synthetic mediation effect that the source did not estimate and would not provide the required native SMD sampling variance.

Likewise, the well-known comparison of flower heads with versus without *Rhinocyllus* is an attack/no-attack contrast, not a more-versus-less synchrony contrast.

IWE therefore does not:

- treat egg load as final reproduction;
- treat attack presence as synchrony;
- multiply the synchrony→egg and egg→seed coefficients;
- bin site-years into high/low synchrony after looking at seed outcomes;
- convert model-predicted seed set into an SMD without source-level group variance.

## Exact unlock condition

This programme would become useful for the frozen replication target if an archived table/raw dataset provides the same site-year or plant-level unit with:

1. source-backed synchrony or a prospectively orderable overlap window;
2. final viable-seed production;
3. enough independent replication and variance to define an SMD-compatible high-versus-low synchrony contrast.

Until that information is recovered, the programme remains a high-value design precedent rather than antagonist cluster #2.

## Relationship to Lamp 1980

Lamp's earlier Platte-thistle dissertation directly showed that seed heads developing before peak oviposition by native seed predators produced the most seeds. That work is ecologically consistent with temporal escape, but three predator species partition seed-head stages and the publicly exposed abstract does not provide a single focal-partner high/low synchrony final-seed contrast with variance.

The Lamp dissertation is therefore useful citation context, not a shortcut around the frozen partner-specific effect contract.
