# Mertensia: temporal service buffering versus resource limitation

Date: 2026-10-08
Studies: Gallagher & Campbell 2020 (DOI 10.1002/ajb2.1439) and
Gallagher & Campbell 2021 (DOI 10.3389/fevo.2021.641693)
System: Mertensia ciliata in the Rocky Mountain Biological Laboratory region
Dependence: DEP_MERTENSIA_GALLAGHER_CAMPBELL_RMBL
Status: cross-experiment ecological mechanism audit, not a new independent
effect cluster or a demonstrated within-plant switch in limiting factor.

## What was observed in 2020

Manipulating flowering onset over four weeks changed pollinator visitation
frequency and composition. Total visits declined more than fivefold, but
seed set showed no significant treatment effect (reported F(3,36)=1.01).

Single-visit tests showed that bumblebees were more effective per visit than
solitary bees or flies, and that the proportion of bumblebee visits rose
later in the season. The authors interpret the increase in visitor quality as
countering the decline in visitor number.

The study does **not** establish seed-set equivalence across weeks: a
non-significant treatment test cannot by itself demonstrate a zero effect.

## What the 2021 water-by-flowering experiment adds

A separate factorial experiment manipulated both water availability and
flowering onset across four weeks, measuring visitation, visitor composition,
seed set and seed mass.

The source reports:

- Seed-set phenology effect: chi-square(df=3)=16.11, p<0.01.
- Seed-set water effect: chi-square(df=2)=11.31, p<0.01.
- Phenology x water interaction on seed set: chi-square(df=6)=1.87,
  not significant.
- Across four weeks, seed set declined by roughly 15%.
- Wet-treatment plants produced on average **40% more seeds per flower**
  than dry-treatment plants; their seeds were also heavier.
- Visitation rate was not significantly associated with seed set after
  accounting for treatment (chi-square=3.14, p=0.08).
- In late week four, wet-treated plants had more seeds despite dry plants
  receiving more visits.

These facts demonstrate that temporal pollinator exposure and final seed
production need not have a one-to-one relationship.

## Ecological mechanism to test

Mertensia flowers have four ovules. The authors note that few effective
visits may suffice to fertilize these ovules, and cite earlier evidence
that seed set saturates as visitation increases.

Two distinct downstream buffers are therefore plausible:

1. **Service substitution:** as overall visits become fewer, more-effective
   visitors replace some less-effective ones.
2. **Limiting-stage conversion:** when pollen delivery is sufficient,
   resources for ovule maturation and seed filling can be more limiting
   than additional visits.

The direct water manipulation in 2021 supports an important plant-resource
pathway to realized seed production. The absence of a significant
visitation-seed-set term and the seed-production advantage of wet-treated
plants in week four support, but do not uniquely prove, a saturated
pollination-service interpretation.

## Why this matters for IWE

A temporal signal need not survive from animal encounter to plant fitness
even when partner activity changes strongly. Its survival depends on the
biological conversion step that is limiting in the focal study.

This is more specific than labelling all non-preserved signals as
"buffered by pollinator composition". The service-quality and post-service
resource pathways are biologically distinct, and the source experiments
do not show that one can replace the other numerically.

**Candidate prediction:** an effective-service timing metric should
predict mature seed number most strongly when pollen delivery is limiting,
but add less explanatory value under pollen saturation or strong abiotic
resource limitation. A test must include seed outcomes and a direct
manipulation/measurement of the limiting stage, not only visitation counts.

## Claim boundary

The 2020 and 2021 papers are different experiments within the same
regional research programme; their contrasts are **not** independent
replications of a universal stage-propagation law.

The 2021 experiment did not detect a phenology-by-water interaction
for seed set. Therefore this source does not show that watering causally
changes the *slope* of timing effects, merely that both phenology and
water independently influence reproductive output.

No new pooled effect is created and no propagation class is reclassified
from this comparison.
