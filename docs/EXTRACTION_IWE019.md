# IWE019 extraction receipt — Ficus pumila × Wiebesia

Source: Liu M et al. 2014. *Competitive Exclusion among Fig Wasps Achieved via Entrainment of Host Plant Flowering Phenology*. PLoS ONE 9:e97783. DOI `10.1371/journal.pone.0097783`.

Status: **context / direct synchrony biology; not strict H1 quantitative effect**.

## What the study directly identifies

The study directly monitored:

- receptive fig phenology on male and female `Ficus pumila`;
- emergence/flight periods of pollinating `Wiebesia` wasps;
- two cryptic pollinator species with strongly separated emergence periods;
- crop maturation/abortion across seasons.

Male receptive crops were timed to pollinator release, and female receptivity differed among sites. Summer female figs could not set seed when entering wasps carried no mature pollen and those figs aborted. The study therefore provides direct biological evidence that timing between pollinator release and receptive figs is critical to seed production.

## Why it is not a strict meta-analysis row

The paper's main response is phenological entrainment, pollinator competitive exclusion and crop cycling. It does not estimate a plant-level or crop-level continuous synchrony/mismatch effect on final seed production with a recoverable sampling variance.

The seasonal statement:

```text
pollinator/pollen unavailable -> female figs aborted
```

is a strong mechanistic observation, but it is not a numerical contrast that can be pooled with Fisher-z/slope/SMD strict-H1 effects without inventing a denominator or sampling error.

The male-fig abortion patterns likewise mix plant sex, crop generation, pollen maturity and pollinator availability. Treating those as a simple synchrony effect would change the estimand.

## Adjudication

IWE019 moves from `unresolved` to `context_only` for strict H1.

It is retained as a high-value mixed/nursery-pollination timing case because it measures the partner-activity side of the chain unusually well.

Together with Silene/Hadena examples, it helps establish a measurement split:

```text
partner timing well measured, final comparable plant-fitness effect missing
```

versus other papers where final fitness is measured but partner timing is not.

## Claim ceiling

The source supports:

> reproductive success in Ficus pumila depends on seasonal alignment between receptive figs, pollen availability and the release of short-lived pollinating wasps.

It does not supply a registered strict-H1 effect size and variance.
