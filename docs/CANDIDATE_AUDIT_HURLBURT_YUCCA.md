# Mixed P1 audit — Hurlburt Yucca programme

Date: 2026-09-25  
Candidate: `MIX002_HURLBURT_2004`  
Primary long-form source: Hurlburt (2004), *Persistence of the moth-yucca mutualism at the northern edge of range*, PhD thesis, University of Alberta, 178–179 pp.

Decision: **retain P1 `blocked_timing_linkage`; public derivative sources show the necessary raw ingredients but not yet the strict overlap-to-final-fitness linkage**

## Why this programme is unusually promising

The northern-range-edge *Yucca glauca × Tegeticula yuccasella* programme was built around the possibility that moth emergence and Soapweed flowering become uncoupled.

Unlike Yucca studies that only compare sites with different moth abundance, Hurlburt's field programme repeatedly monitored both partners during the same flowering seasons.

For Onefour, Alberta, during 1999–2003:

- adult Yucca Moths were counted in approximately 100 fresh flowers several times per week during flowering;
- individually marked clones and inflorescences were followed to determine fruit set;
- each year approximately 30–200 mature fruits per population were dissected;
- fruit, oviposition, larval and moth-density indices were retained by year.

This is the correct programme architecture for IWE.

## Why this is not yet a strict synchrony effect

The public tables provide annual moth density and annual reproductive performance, but **annual moth abundance is not phenological synchrony**.

The strict exposure requires the timing relation between:

- the within-season flowering/receptive-flower curve; and
- the within-season adult-moth emergence/activity curve.

The public status reports state that moth counts were made several times per week and that flowering at the northern edge is asynchronous and can fail to coincide with moth emergence. They do not expose the actual date-resolved pair of curves needed to calculate or prospectively order yearly overlap.

Therefore IWE will not use:

- higher annual moths/flower as “more synchrony”;
- fruit production itself as evidence of matching;
- Pinhorn absence of moths/fruit as a phenological contrast;
- pooled 1999–2003 viable-seed variance as if it were a year-specific high/low-overlap variance.

## Exact unlock condition

The Hurlburt programme can close mixed #2 if the 2004 thesis or archived field data provide, for at least two independent biological groups/windows within the programme:

1. flowering timing or receptive-flower availability by date;
2. contemporaneous adult *T. yuccasella* activity/emergence by date;
3. a source-backed high-versus-low overlap ordering independent of final fitness;
4. final viable-seed/fruit output with an SMD-compatible variance at the corresponding biological unit.

A year-specific overlap index combined with year-specific final reproduction could also be useful, but a continuous slope would remain outside the frozen SMD replication target unless the source itself supplies an admissible group contrast.

## Relationship to other Yucca leads

- **Rentería / Yucca filifera**: within-season partner activity and final seed fate are both measured, but mature seed records lack flowering-cohort identity.
- **Dodd & Linhart / Yucca glauca**: reproductive outcomes and moth abundance vary along elevation, but the focal contrast is spatial abundance/environment, not temporal synchrony.
- **Hurlburt / Yucca glauca**: the field design actually collected repeated within-season adult-moth counts during flowering and final reproduction, making it the strongest Yucca archival lead if the date-resolved series can be recovered.

## Retrieval status

The thesis is repeatedly cited by Canadian federal/provincial recovery documents, but an openly retrievable full dissertation was not located in the current search.

Until the thesis/data are recovered, the candidate remains `blocked_timing_linkage`, P1, rather than being promoted from annual abundance summaries.
