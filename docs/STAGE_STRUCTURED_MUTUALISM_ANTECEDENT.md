# Stage-structured mutualism antecedent audit

Date: 2026-10-06  
Status: novelty guardrail for the IWE pivot; this document forbids overclaiming stage structure itself as novel.

## Established antecedent: ontogenetic antagonism–mutualism coupling

Ke & Nakazawa 2018, *Oikos* 127:353–363, DOI `10.1111/oik.04702`, develops a theoretical framework for **ontogenetic antagonism–mutualism coupling**.

Its motivating biological architecture already includes pollinating insects in which:

- juvenile/larval stages are herbivores or otherwise antagonistic;
- adult stages act as pollinators and are mutualistic.

The paper asks how an ontogenetic change in interaction type affects the resilience of stage-structured communities.

This directly predates any IWE claim that one lineage can switch between antagonism and mutualism across life stages.

## Established antecedent: stage-structured mutualism

Nakazawa 2020, *Oikos*, DOI `10.1111/oik.06653`, explicitly argues for a broader **stage-structured mutualism** perspective.

The paper notes that flowering plants and pollinating insects undergo ontogenetic niche shifts and that pollinating insects may be antagonistic as larvae while mutualistic as adults. It develops community modules including:

- inter-stage partner sharing;
- ontogenetic antagonism–mutualism coupling;
- ontogenetic partner shift.

Thus the general stage-structured architecture is already an established theoretical object.

## Additional antecedent: mismatch buffering

Weir & Phillimore 2024, *Global Change Biology*, DOI `10.1111/gcb.17294`, synthesizes mechanisms that buffer phenological mismatch by:

1. reducing asynchrony;
2. reducing the performance cost of asynchrony;
3. damping performance variation at higher organizational levels.

IWE therefore must not claim novelty for the existence of compensation or buffering either.

## Claims explicitly prohibited for IWE

Do not write that IWE is the first to show or propose:

- that one interaction can switch sign among life stages;
- that adult pollinators can have antagonistic larvae;
- that stage structure matters in mutualisms;
- that ontogenetic niche shifts couple antagonism and mutualism;
- that phenological mismatch can be buffered;
- that changing partner identity/effectiveness can compensate for lower encounter rate.

Those ideas already have clear antecedents.

## Candidate IWE contribution after these exclusions

The remaining candidate contribution is **temporal propagation through a stage-structured interaction**.

IWE asks a different question:

> Given a source-defined temporal signal at one interaction stage, what happens to that signal before final plant fitness is expressed?

The current transformation ledger distinguishes:

- preserved;
- shifted / filtered;
- sign reversed;
- erased;
- buffered;
- tracking inertia;
- final direction preserved while mechanism changes.

The key variable is therefore not merely stage identity, but the **phase relation and causal distance among stages**.

Examples already present in IWE include:

- *Silene–Hadena*: year-to-year change in flower/egg-to-larva phase relation accompanies reversal in the synchrony–predation sign;
- *Cardamine–Anthocharis*: total egg exposure and the subset capable of producing later damaging larvae occupy displaced temporal windows;
- *Glochidion–Epicephala*: adult service and larval seed cost are separated by roughly eight months;
- *Mertensia*: a >5-fold decline in visitation is buffered before seed set by seasonal change in pollinator composition/effectiveness;
- *Posledovich* developmental-race experiment: stage matching affects consumer performance but is erased at the chosen mature-seedpod plant endpoint.

## Current safe novelty statement

A manuscript-safe formulation is:

> Building on stage-structured mutualism, phenological mismatch, developmental-race and buffering theory, we track how source-defined temporal information is transformed across interaction stages and ask whether its direction survives to final plant fitness.

This should remain the default wording unless a systematic novelty search demonstrates a stronger priority claim.

## Remaining novelty test

Before submission, the literature search must explicitly test whether another framework already:

1. treats phenological effects as signals propagated through multiple causal stages;
2. classifies direction-preserving versus direction-changing transformations;
3. relates the provenance/causal position of the timing measurement to downstream fitness retention;
4. compares simpler upstream timing with prospectively defined effective-stage timing within the same final-fitness dataset.

Absence from the current targeted search is not proof that such a synthesis does not exist.
