# Posledovich 2015 — developmental matching changes herbivore performance but not the mature-seedpod escape endpoint

Date: 2026-10-03  
Study: Posledovich et al. 2015, *Anthocharis cardamines* × five Brassicaceae hosts  
DOI: `10.1111/1365-2656.12417`  
Landscape role: prospective boundary test of the stage-specific window hypothesis.

## Experimental design

The experiment directly manipulated two quantities that the stage-specific IWE model treats as mechanistically important:

1. **host phenological state at oviposition**;
2. **temperature during subsequent larval and plant development**.

Mated female *A. cardamines* laid one egg on potted host plants presented at different reproductive stages.

Egg-bearing plants were then randomly assigned to three temperature treatments, approximately 13, 15 and 17 C.

The design therefore changes both the initial host-consumer phase relation and the subsequent relative developmental rates.

## Herbivore side responds strongly to stage alignment

Across most host species, larvae performed better on hosts that were at earlier reproductive stages when oviposition occurred.

Higher developmental temperature also:

- shortened larval development;
- increased pupal mass in several hosts;
- changed the probability that larvae needed a second host plant.

For *Cardamine pratensis*, host phenological state and temperature interacted: the relative rates of larval versus plant development changed whether a larva exhausted the first host or whether the plant matured beyond suitable feeding stages.

This is a direct experimental demonstration of a developmental race.

## Plant mature-seedpod endpoint does not follow the same predictors

The experiment also measured whether the first host plant **outgrew the larva and formed mature seedpods that were no longer suitable for larval feeding**.

For that plant-side endpoint, the source reports a different result:

> the probability of a plant outgrowing a larva depended only on host species identity.

Phenological state at oviposition and developmental temperature did not explain this mature-seedpod escape probability in the fitted analysis.

Thus stage alignment strongly matters to the herbivore, but does not automatically translate into the measured final plant escape endpoint.

## Why this is a crucial boundary

A simple stage-specific prediction would be:

`better plant-consumer phase alignment -> stronger herbivore performance -> lower final plant reproduction`.

This experiment shows that the first arrow can be strong while the final plant endpoint remains dominated by species-level plant traits.

Therefore:

> moving from calendar timing to stage-specific timing is biologically meaningful, but it is not guaranteed to improve prediction of every plant-fitness endpoint.

The value of the stage-specific model must be demonstrated against final plant reproduction rather than inferred from larval performance.

## Relation to IWE032

The species *A. cardamines* also appears in IWE032, but the programmes answer different questions.

- Posledovich 2015 experimentally manipulates host stage and temperature across five host species and directly tests the developmental race.
- Davies & Saccheri 2024 reconstructs realized egg exposure, active future-damage exposure and final intact reproductive units in a natural *Cardamine pratensis* population.

The laboratory developmental-race result is therefore an independent boundary test, not another Dibbinsdale dependence cluster.

## Claim boundary

The plant response is mature-seedpod formation / host outgrowth, not a count of viable seeds per plant.

It is nevertheless a post-herbivory mature reproductive endpoint and is retained as `boundary_evidence`.

It must not be counted among programmes supporting stage-alignment superiority.
