# Candidate audit — Thymus decussatus × Pseudophilotes sinaicus

Date: 2026-09-28  
Study ID: `IWE033`  
Source: Thompson & Gilbert (2014), *Basic and Applied Ecology* 15:353–361  
DOI: `10.1016/j.baae.2014.05.003`  
Decision: **context-only; direct synchrony evidence but no plant reproductive endpoint**

## Why this source entered the screen

The study directly tests spatial and temporal phenological synchrony between:

- flowering of Sinai Thyme, *Thymus decussatus*; and
- flight timing and abundance of the specialist Sinai Baton Blue butterfly, *Pseudophilotes sinaicus*.

The butterfly larvae feed exclusively on host buds and flowers, so this is a genuine antagonist timing interaction rather than an occurrence-only association.

The study reports substantial interannual variation in host flowering and compares that phenology with butterfly flight timing among patches and years.

## Why it does not enter Tier A

The IWE Tier-A estimand requires a quantitative **plant reproductive or lifetime-fitness endpoint** matched to the timing exposure.

The 2014 study is centered on:

- plant flowering phenology;
- butterfly flight timing and abundance;
- synchrony/asynchrony between those components; and
- possible consequences for butterfly population dynamics.

The accessible article record does not report a plant seed-set, fruit-set, fecundity, viable-seed, or lifetime-fitness response attached to the synchrony contrast.

Therefore the paper cannot provide a strict plant-fitness effect for IWE even though its partner timing measurement is unusually direct.

## Gate status

| gate | status |
|---|---|
| direct plant timing | yes |
| direct animal timing/activity | yes |
| same-season synchrony architecture | yes |
| plant reproductive/lifetime fitness | **no** |
| Tier-A quantitative effect | **no** |
| screening decision | **context_only** |

## Interpretation

This is a useful negative control for the IWE evidence architecture.

It demonstrates that a paper can have excellent plant–herbivore synchrony data yet still fail the strict plant-fitness estimand because the biological response is measured on the animal side.

No attempt should be made to convert butterfly abundance or population dynamics into plant reproductive performance.
