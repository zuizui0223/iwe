# IWE012 Valdés & Ehrlén 2017 — seed predators reverse flowering-time selection

Date: 2026-10-07  
Study: *Gentiana pneumonanthe × Phengaris alcon*  
DOI: `10.1002/ecy.1633`  
Propagation role: antagonist stage reverses the seasonal flowering-time fitness direction.

## Source pattern

Across 20 populations and two years, flowering-time selection differs according to presence of the specialist seed predator.

Without the butterfly, selection favors earlier flowering.

Where *P. alcon* is present, caterpillars preferentially attack early-flowering plants and the direction of selection shifts toward later flowering.

The source therefore identifies the antagonist as the biological agent producing the shift.

## Propagation state

The link is classified as **sign_reversed**:

`baseline early-flowering advantage -> concentrated early attack -> final selection favouring later flowering`.

The upstream reference is `seasonal_position_only`; the study does not independently reconstruct adult butterfly availability as the timing coordinate.

## Claim boundary

This is a selection-surface result rather than a strict partner-synchrony effect.

No adult-activity curve is inferred from attack, and no new common effect size is created.
