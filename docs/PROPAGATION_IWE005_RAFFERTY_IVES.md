# IWE005 Rafferty–Ives 2012 — experimental flowering shifts propagate to mature seed set

Date: 2026-10-07  
Study: Rafferty & Ives 2012, *Tradescantia ohiensis* and *Asclepias incarnata*  
DOI: `10.1890/11-0967.1`  
Propagation role: prospective direct-timing manipulation carried to mature seed production.

## Design

Flowering onset was manipulated in greenhouses so plants entered the field across five weekly phenological treatments spanning roughly two weeks before to two weeks after the contemporary first-bloom date.

Focal flowers were exposed until a single insect visit and then returned to controlled conditions. Fruits were allowed to ripen and **mature seeds produced after that visit were counted**. Flowers that failed to set fruit were assigned seed set = 0.

Thus the chain is prospectively defined:

`experimental flowering timing -> pollinator assemblage/effectiveness -> mature seed set after a visit`.

The timing treatment is not defined from the reproductive outcome.

## Tradescantia ohiensis

The focal-flower GLMM gives a positive week effect on seed set:

- week coefficient = **+0.41 ± 0.15 SE** on the log scale;
- z = **2.76**;
- **p = 0.006**.

Later experimental flowering therefore produces more mature seeds after a pollinator visit.

Pollinator composition changes across weeks, and several pollinator taxa also become more effective later in the season.

The source reports that a model including temporal pollinator effects explains **84%** of the variation in seed set among phenological treatments.

The signal is classified as **preserved**:

`later experimental flowering -> higher pollinator effectiveness / realized seed set -> higher mature seed set`.

## Asclepias incarnata

For *A. incarnata*, seed set per visit also increases with later flowering onset.

The temporal mechanism is primarily turnover in the pollinator assemblage rather than strong within-taxon changes:

- aggregated pollinator importance alone explains **80%** of variation in seed set among weeks;
- adding temporal changes in pollinator effectiveness increases this to **90%**.

Expected successful-pollination rate, combining visitation, pollinator composition and taxon-specific effectiveness, is lowest near the early/current flowering window and highest two weeks later.

The propagation state is again **preserved** at the mature-seed endpoint.

## Important qualification

This is not evidence that partner abundance alone explains the response.

For *T. ohiensis*, hand-pollinated controls also show some temporal change in seed-set potential, although the focal-flower week effect remains after the control terms are included in the model.

For *A. incarnata*, floral nectar quantity/quality changes through time and may influence visitor behaviour.

The source therefore supports a prospective timing signal that reaches mature seed set, but the downstream mechanism includes both changing pollinator composition/effectiveness and changing plant traits.

This is precisely why the propagation ledger records **signal preservation**, not a claim that one isolated causal mediator explains the entire effect.
