## What the public record now establishes

The Hurlburt Onefour programme is not a vague biological near-miss. Public Canadian status material explicitly documents the measurement architecture needed for a strict mixed-system candidate:

- during Soapweed flowering in 1999–2003, all Yucca Moths in approximately 100 fresh flowers across Onefour were counted several times per week;
- a subset of individually marked clones and inflorescences was followed to fruit set;
- 30–200 mature fruit were dissected annually for oviposition and larval outcomes;
- the public synthesis reports annual indices including fruit/inflorescence, fruit/clone, moths/flower, ovipositions/fruit and larvae/fruit.

This strongly supports that contemporaneous partner activity and later plant reproduction were measured inside the same long-running programme.

## What is actually public

### 2013 COSEWIC synthesis

The 2013 Canadian assessment exposes annual summaries for Onefour. For example, its Yucca Moth table reports year-level fruit and moth indices for 1999–2003, with some values explicitly attributed to Hurlburt 2004.

These are useful provenance checks, but they collapse the season to a year-level aggregate. They do not expose the dates of the repeated flower/moth censuses or a flowering-cohort/clone grouping that can be ordered prospectively by measured moth overlap and then carried into final plant reproduction.

### 2004 conference abstracts

Two public 2004 conference records were recovered:

1. Prairie Conservation and Endangered Species Conference proceedings, “Strategies for Survival: Persistence of the Moth-Yucca Mutualism at the Northern Edge of Range”.
2. Entomological Society of Canada Bulletin, Graduate Student Symposium abstract by Donna D. Hurlburt and John Addicott.

Both confirm that moth population dynamics/behaviour were studied and summarize the northern-edge biological result. Neither contains a date-resolved flowering-by-moth table, marked-unit final fruit/seed table, group means/variance, or another extractable strict-SMD surface.

### Thesis availability

Multiple authoritative Canadian sources identify the underlying dissertation as:

> Hurlburt, D. D. 2004. *Persistence of the mutualism between Yuccas and Moths at the northern edge of range*. Ph.D. thesis, University of Alberta, Edmonton, Alberta. ~179 pp.

Targeted public searches by exact title, author, institution and thesis terms did not recover the dissertation text or an institutional-repository copy. The public Boyce Lab alumni page confirms Hurlburt's 2004 Ph.D., but does not expose the thesis.

## Why the annual public table cannot be promoted

The public annual series is not a substitute for the frozen IWE timing estimand.

A contrast such as “year with more moths/flower versus year with fewer moths/flower” would measure annual partner abundance, not whether an early/late plant flowering window was more or less synchronized with the contemporaneous partner activity curve.

Likewise, correlating annual moths/flower with annual fruit/clone would:

- change the exposure from phenological synchrony to abundance;
- use years rather than source-defined plant timing groups;
- ignore annual environmental differences;
- and manufacture an effect form not reported or preregistered.

The annual aggregates therefore remain context/provenance only.

## Exact unlock datum

Hurlburt advances only if the thesis or original programme tables expose enough of the following to make a source-defined contrast:

1. date-stamped flowering/receptive-flower observations;
2. date-stamped adult *Tegeticula yuccasella* activity from the same season;
3. a source-backed link from those dates to marked clone/inflorescence (or another defensible independent biological unit);
4. final post-cost plant reproduction for the same units, ideally fruit production and/or viable seeds after moth larval consumption;
5. variance-bearing group summaries or unit-level data sufficient for the native SMD contract.

The high-versus-low overlap groups must be definable from the timing data before looking at the final reproductive response.

## Operational consequence

Public-web retrieval for this candidate is now closed. The next action is not another generic search. It is a long-form/archive retrieval:

- obtain the 2004 University of Alberta dissertation through a library/institutional route; or
- recover archived Onefour field tables / author-held data containing the date-to-marked-unit linkage.

Until that object is recovered, `MIX002_HURLBURT_2004` remains P1 `blocked_timing_linkage` and contributes zero strict-H1 effects.
