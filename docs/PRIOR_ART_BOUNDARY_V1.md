# IWE prior-art boundary v1

Date: 2026-09-17
Status: active novelty boundary during systematic screening

## Nearest quantitative synthesis

Munguía-Rosas et al. (2011), *Meta-analysis of phenotypic selection on flowering phenology suggests that early flowering plants are favoured* (Ecology Letters 14:511–521; DOI `10.1111/j.1461-0248.2011.01601.x`) is the closest prior quantitative synthesis.

Its main data sets contain 87 species for flowering time and 18 species for flowering synchrony. It tests associations between plant flowering phenology and fitness, and evaluates moderators including pollinator dependence, reproductive duration, latitude and longevity.

Critically for IWE, the supporting material also contains dedicated random-effects summaries of **pre-dispersal seed predation on flowering time**, in addition to selection on flowering time, selection on flowering synchrony and plant-size effects on flowering time. Thus IWE must not claim novelty for either:

```text
flowering time -> fitness meta-analysis
```

or

```text
pre-dispersal seed predation associated with flowering time
```

as generic questions.

The publisher lists:

- `ELE_1601_sm_appendixS1.xls` — species/family list, effect sizes and moderator variables;
- `ELE_1601_sm_appendixS2.xls` — authorship, titles and original data sources;
- supplementary forest/funnel figures.

These files define a finite backward-search source universe for IWE even if publisher access prevents direct automated retrieval in the present environment.

## IWE's narrower estimand

IWE requires a different measurement object:

```text
plant-partner temporal matching
        -> final plant reproductive / fitness outcome
```

where the plant and focal interaction partner are both temporally measured at compatible biological grain.

This differs from:

```text
calendar flowering date -> fitness
conspecific flowering synchrony -> fitness
flowering date -> seed-predation rate
seed-predator presence/damage -> selection on flowering date
```

unless the source also identifies partner timing sufficiently to construct a declared overlap/mismatch exposure.

## Interaction-role comparison

IWE additionally asks whether the reproductive consequence of the **same timing estimand** differs among:

- mutualists;
- antagonists;
- mixed pollinating seed predators.

The novelty claim is therefore conditional on recovering comparable partner-timing evidence. If such evidence remains sparse outside mutualisms, the defensible result becomes an evidence-architecture result rather than a three-class biological-effect estimate.

## Nearby conceptual reviews

Elzinga et al. (2007) synthesizes how pollinators, herbivores and seed predators shape flowering phenology and is a required backward-search seed. It motivates counteracting temporal selection but does not substitute for IWE's source-level timing-to-final-fitness extraction.

Later work also demonstrates particular mechanisms such as:

- temporal escape from seed predators;
- pollinator-mediated selection on flowering date;
- non-monotone antagonist fitness surfaces;
- nursery-pollination benefit/cost trade-offs;
- climate-mediated variation in interaction intensity.

IWE does not claim to originate any of these mechanisms.

## Claim rule

The paper may claim novelty only at the narrowest supported level, for example:

> We systematically evaluated whether published studies identify the effect of plant–interaction-partner temporal matching on final plant reproduction using a common timing estimand, and whether comparable evidence exists across mutualistic, antagonistic and mixed interactions.

It must not claim:

> This is the first meta-analysis of flowering phenology and fitness.

or:

> This is the first synthesis showing that seed predators select on flowering phenology.

## Search consequence

Munguía-Rosas Appendix S2 and the Elzinga review reference set are part of the frozen finite backward-search universe. Records recovered from them are screened under the same IWE criteria; they are not automatically admitted because they contributed to an earlier meta-analysis.
