# IWE035 extraction receipt — Pilson 2000

Source: Pilson D. 2000. *Herbivory and natural selection on flowering phenology in wild sunflower, Helianthus annuus*. Oecologia 122:72–82. DOI `10.1007/PL00008838`.

Status: **unresolved strict-antagonist candidate; no quantitative IWE effect admitted yet**.

## Why this study is high priority

The source directly documents, in the same wild-sunflower study population:

- genetic variation in mean flowering date;
- seasonal patterns of five seed-feeding herbivores;
- plant damage by those herbivores;
- plant fitness;
- multivariate phenotypic selection analyses with and without focal herbivore damage terms.

The seasonal enemy patterns are role-specific rather than a generic calendar effect:

- `Haplorhynchites aeneus`, `Homoeosoma electellum`, and `Suleima helianthana` damage is highest early and declines across the flowering season;
- `Gymnocarena diffusa` damage is lowest early and increases later;
- `Smicronyx fulvus/sordidus` damage is approximately constant through the flowering period.

Damage by `Haplorhynchites`, `Homoeosoma`, and `Suleima` is reported as detrimental to plant fitness.

## Mediation result recovered from the source

Two selection analyses are reported.

1. When `Homoeosoma` and `Suleima` damage is included together with flowering date and plant traits, directional selection acts against herbivore damage but no independent directional selection on flowering date is detected.
2. When the two damage terms are omitted, later flowering is significantly favored.

The source interprets the comparison as evidence that apparent selection for later flowering is attributable to seed-herbivore damage: later plants escape the enemies whose attack declines across the season.

## Why this is not yet an H1 effect

IWE H1 requires a recoverable study-level effect of plant–partner temporal matching on final plant reproductive performance, together with sampling variance.

The currently recovered accessible source material establishes the causal/mediation logic and seasonal enemy pattern, but does not expose a numeric coefficient + standard error (or raw individual-level data) that can be converted without additional assumptions into IWE's common synchrony effect.

In particular, IWE will not:

- treat the coefficient for flowering date from a model that omits herbivory as a direct plant–enemy synchrony effect;
- infer a sampling variance from a P-value category;
- pool damage-mediated selection with directly measured overlap–fitness correlations without a registered conversion;
- choose only the early-declining enemy taxa after viewing their effects while ignoring the enemy whose damage increases later or the approximately constant seed-weevil damage.

## Evidence role

Until source-level quantitative recovery succeeds, IWE035 is retained as:

- `unresolved_strict` antagonist evidence;
- high-information direct seasonal-enemy evidence;
- a benchmark showing that the antagonist literature sometimes identifies timing through mediation rather than an explicit overlap index.

## Next recovery routes

Admissible routes are, in order:

1. original article tables containing coefficient/SE or enough test statistics for exact reconstruction;
2. archived raw data or supplementary data;
3. author-provided data sufficient to reconstruct flowering date, enemy exposure and final fitness;
4. no extraction, with the study retained as unresolved/context if none of the above exists.

No numeric value will be reverse-engineered from plotted significance alone.
