# Collin & Shykoff 2010 — a realized mixed fitness landscape in Dianthus–Hadena

Date: 2026-10-03  
Study: Collin & Shykoff 2010, *Dianthus sylvestris × Hadena compta*  
DOI: `10.1007/s11258-009-9620-5`  
Landscape role: independent mixed-system final-fitness evidence on a realized seasonal interaction coordinate.

## Why this study becomes usable after the pivot

Under the original strict-H1 contract, the study is correctly rejected as a partner-synchrony effect because it does not provide an independent contemporaneous adult *H. compta* activity curve.

The study does, however, follow the complete seasonal reproductive surface at the individual-plant level:

- flowering onset;
- fruit set;
- secondary predation by itinerant *Hadena* caterpillars;
- mature seeds remaining after predation;
- relative and absolute reproductive success.

This is exactly the kind of **realized interaction-window** evidence the pivot was designed to retain without relabelling it as independent adult availability.

## Final plant reproductive endpoint

The source defines relative reproductive success as:

> the percentage of flowers that set unattacked fruits containing mature seeds available for dispersal.

Thus the response is downstream of both pollination and secondary larval predation.

Relative reproductive success declines across flowering onset in both study years:

- **2001:** slope = **−0.037 ± 0.009 SE**;
- **2003:** slope = **−0.076 ± 0.030 SE**.

The time effect is significant in the source ANOVA:

- 2001: F = **14.24**, p < 0.001;
- 2003: F = **5.62**, p < 0.05.

This gives an independent final-fitness mixed landscape rather than only a benefit or cost intermediate.

## Benefit and cost channels change differently through the season

The source separately tracks fruit set and secondary seed predation.

### 2001

Flowering onset affects both:

- fruit set: F = **7.01**, p < 0.01;
- secondary predation: F = **8.17**, p < 0.01.

Plants flowering later show poorer final reproductive success because the positive and negative channels jointly deteriorate.

### 2003

The channel geometry differs.

- linear fruit-set time effect: F = **2.49**, not significant;
- secondary predation time effect: F = **8.02**, p < 0.01;
- quadratic secondary-predation effect: F = **6.81**, p < 0.05.

Predation peaks around mid-season, yet net relative reproductive success still declines over flowering onset.

Thus similar final seasonal slopes can arise from different underlying benefit/cost geometries.

## Stage structure

The study distinguishes two seed-predation routes:

1. primary predation associated with eggs/young larvae in flowers or very young fruits;
2. secondary predation by itinerant later caterpillars attacking maturing fruits and consuming developing seeds.

At the flower level in 2001, egg receipt / primary predation risk increases over the flowering season, while later secondary predation can empty developing fruits.

This again shows why a single label such as "Hadena synchrony" compresses multiple stages.

## Interpretation

The key result is not that later flowering is universally bad.

It is that in one independent nursery-pollination programme, **calendar flowering onset maps onto a final post-predation fitness surface whose component processes differ between years**.

This adds a third independent mixed programme to the broadened landscape evidence:

- IWE014: benefit-cost channel decoupling;
- IWE015 / Mountain Lake: high-provenance mixed anchor, variance held;
- Dianthus-Hadena: realized seasonal net-fitness landscape.

Only IWE015 has an independently measured adult focal-partner window. The provenance classes remain separate.

## Claim boundary

This component must not be promoted to strict H1 because:

- no independent focal-season adult *Hadena* availability curve orders the flowering groups;
- flowering onset is a calendar coordinate;
- realized predation is an interaction outcome, not adult partner availability.

It is therefore registered as `realized_window_evidence`, with the seasonal cost surface providing the realized interaction reference.
