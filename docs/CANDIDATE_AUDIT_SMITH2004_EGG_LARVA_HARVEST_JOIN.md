# Smith et al. 2004 — same-plot egg, larval and harvest-damage conversion route

Date: 2026-10-08
Source: Smith, Lamb, Wise & Olfert (2004), *An interspersed refuge for Sitodiplosis mosellana and a biocontrol agent Macroglenes penetrans to manage crop resistance in wheat*.
DOI: `10.1079/BER2004291`
Status: **targeted source-data candidate only**, not a newly fitted IWE final-fitness effect.

## What the publication establishes

Eight replicated spring-wheat field experiments varied the proportion of susceptible refuge plants mixed with Sm1-resistant wheat (source-defined mixtures include 0, 5, 10, 15 and 100%, or 0, 5 and 100%).

The authors explicitly measured within the same plots:

- midge egg frequencies on spikes;
- mature midge larval frequencies;
- parasitized larvae;
- midge-affected seeds in the harvested crop.

This is unusually relevant to IWE's *conversion-bottleneck* question because early exposure, survival/suppression and terminal crop damage share an experimental plot unit.

The abstract reports:

- mature larvae from resistant wheat: **2% or fewer**, versus about **80%** in susceptible wheat;
- egg deposition on resistant wheat: **65% lower** than on susceptible wheat;
- an interspersed 5% susceptible refuge produced about **41 mature larvae per mature larva** from resistant plants.

These are source estimates, not reconstructed new treatment effects.

## Ecological question it could test

Does the terminal number of surviving larvae adequately capture prior exposure's reproductive damage, or do egg/early-attack histories retain explanatory value after larval survival is measured?

One prediction is that Sm1 antibiosis changes the egg-to-adult conversion so strongly that egg number and mature-larva number carry different information about final damaged seeds.

The question is motivated independently by Chavalle et al. 2015, which reports yield gains after midge suppression even in a resistant cultivar and proposes young-larva injury or costly plant defence as competing mechanisms.

## Exact data and time-gate needed

The published abstract verifies common **plot grain**, but does not expose the numeric plot table or prove when each intermediate observation occurred relative to grain harvest.

A valid quantitative reconstruction requires, for each trial/plot and cultivar mixture:

1. egg count with a source-verified observation date;
2. mature-larva and parasitoid count with an observation date;
3. harvested midge-affected seed count/percentage, with consistent denominator;
4. replication / experiment / year identifiers and assigned resistant/susceptible mixture;
5. any relevant host-stage / adult-flight observation if making a phenological timing claim.

Compare only source-supported groups: egg-only versus mature-larva-only versus source-defined egg × survival or parasitoid filters, controlling for assigned mixture. If intermediate larvae are assessed only at or after the harvest sample, such a model is **retrospective explanation, not forecasting**. Outer validation should hold out independent experiments or years, not nested spikes.
