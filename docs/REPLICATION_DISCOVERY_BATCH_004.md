# Replication discovery batch 004 — mixed-system audit

Date: 2026-09-25  
Status: candidate adjudication complete; strict mixed #2 still unresolved

## Purpose

After mutualist replication closed at two independent SMD clusters and antagonist search narrowed to the *Astragalus–Tomares* one-blocker candidate, this batch focused exclusively on mixed pollinating seed-consuming systems outside the already represented *Silene stellata–Hadena ectypa* programme.

The frozen requirement remains:

`same-season partner activity -> plant timing overlap -> final post-cost plant reproduction -> SMD-compatible variance`.

## Chamaerops humilis × Derelomus chamaeropsis

Source: Jácome-Flores et al. 2018, *Annals of Botany*, DOI `10.1093/aob/mcx177`.

This study is unusually strong on host flowering synchrony: anthesing inflorescences were censused repeatedly over three seasons and a flowering-synchrony index predicts nursery-pollinator larval occupancy.

It is nevertheless not a qualifying IWE mixed seed-predator programme.

The life cycle is sex-specific. Adult weevils pollinate both sexes, but larvae develop primarily inside persistent **male inflorescence rachises**. Successful fruit development in female inflorescences largely prevents larval development. The measured plant payment is therefore not the same final female seed/fruit surface required by the `mixed_pollinating_seed_predator` target.

Decision: `rejected_other`.

## Rheum nobile × Bradysia sp.

Primary temporal source: Song et al. 2016, *Scientific Reports* 6:29886, DOI `10.1038/srep29886`.

This is a genuine pollinating seed-consuming mutualism. Female Bradysia pollinate while ovipositing and each surviving larva consumes the single seed of its fruit.

The temporal experiment follows flowers opening on successive days and later scores fruit set, abortion and seed predation. The critical contract problem is explicit in the source: **seed predation rate is used as a substitute for oviposition rate** in the temporal analysis.

That makes the proposed synchrony exposure a downstream damage outcome rather than an independently measured partner-activity window. Direct adult-visitor observations exist in the programme, but they do not supply the same-year/window ordering for the 2015 temporal experiment.

Also, because larva-bearing fruits lose the seed entirely, the temporal component does not supply a direct variance-bearing net seed output after consumption.

Decision: `rejected_timing`.

## Senita cactus × senita moth

Sources include Holland & Fleming 1999 and Holland & Fleming 2002; focal phenology DOI `10.1007/s00442-002-1061-y`.

This is an excellent mixed system biologically:

- female *Upiga virescens* actively pollinate and oviposit;
- larvae consume developing seeds and fruit;
- fruit survivorship and mature fruit production are measured;
- six years of flowering onset and seasonal phenology are documented.

However, the long-term seasonal analysis asks primarily when diurnal bee co-pollinators can access flowers. Plant flowering onset and flower-closing time are quantified, but no independently measured seasonal *Upiga* activity curve is used to order plant timing windows by moth synchrony.

Statements that moths were rare or abundant, and fruit set under moth-only access, cannot be recycled into the missing exposure.

Decision: `rejected_timing` for strict H1.

## Dichaea cogniauxiana × Montella spp.

Source: Nunes et al. 2018, *Current Biology*, DOI `10.1016/j.cub.2018.02.013`.

The study provides unusually rich nursery-pollination data: active pollination, oviposition, fruit abortion, larval seed/ovule consumption, fruit biomass, parasitoid rescue, plant identity and multiple years.

The missing piece is again the IWE exposure. Figure 2 describes interannual infestation dynamics, not flowering-time overlap with an independently measured Montella activity curve. No strict within-season more-versus-less synchrony contrast is recovered.

Decision: `rejected_timing`.

## Sambucus sieboldiana × Heterhelus — P1 linkage candidate

Source: Kawashima et al. 2026, *Plants, People, Planet*, DOI `10.1002/ppp3.70175`.

This is the strongest new lead in batch 004.

The study directly establishes that:

- Heterhelus beetles dominate effective floral visitation;
- beetles are abundant during anthesis and decline as flowers senesce;
- mating and oviposition occur on flowers;
- larvae enter fertilized ovaries and consume developing seeds;
- larval infestation drives fruit abortion;
- final fruit set, net fruit set, seed set, seed weight, viability, germination and consumed-versus-viable seeds are quantified;
- supplementary raw data are openly described at inflorescence/fruit level.

The supporting workbook contains:

- Dataset S4: fruit set and net fruit set per inflorescence;
- Dataset S10: fate of flowers/fruits per inflorescence through pollination, abortion and infestation;
- Table S1 in the supporting PDF: dates and durations of direct visitor observations.

The unresolved question is **linkage**. The main text does not establish that each reproductive inflorescence in S4/S10 can be assigned to a same-season beetle-activity window or overlap value. Qualitative abundance decline through floral senescence is not enough to manufacture a high/low synchrony SMD.

Decision: `blocked_timing_linkage`, P1.

### Exact unlock condition

Promote only if the supplementary identifiers permit a source-backed mapping such as:

`site/year/inflorescence flowering window -> contemporaneous Heterhelus abundance -> final net viable-seed or mature-fruit outcome`

at an independent biological-unit grain.

If the visitor observations are only site/date aggregates with no compatible reproductive-unit linkage, the study remains context/mechanism evidence and does not close mixed #2.

## Current search state

The mixed search now has two complementary P1 programmes:

1. **Bopp/Gottsberger *Silene–Hadena*** — timing complete, final plant fitness missing from the article; recover Bopp 2003 long-form source.
2. **Kawashima et al. *Sambucus–Heterhelus*** — partner observations and final reproduction both present, but timing-to-fitness linkage is not yet demonstrated; audit the open supplementary workbook.

This is more informative than broadening to additional taxa. The next work should try to close either one missing link before another generic nursery-pollination search.
